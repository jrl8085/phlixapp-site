Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 24, 2026

Overview

Phlix ("the App") is an independent photo and video app that works with a Plex Media Server that you own, operate, or are authorized to access. This Privacy Policy explains what information the App processes, how it is processed, and what choices you have. By using the App you agree to the processing described below; if you do not agree, do not use the App.

The Short Version

Phlix collects anonymous product-interaction data (which features are used, where things break) to improve the app. This data is not linked to your identity and is not used for advertising or tracking. Phlix does not collect your name, email, photos, library contents, server name, server address, or Plex account information. You can opt out of analytics entirely in Settings → Privacy. App Store purchases are recorded with RevenueCat, which that setting does not turn off; see "Network Communication" below. If you turn on Camera Upload (optional, beta, from version 1.0.21), the App copies the photos and videos on your device to your own server over your home network, and never to the developer; see "Camera Upload" below. This website (phlixapp.com) uses Google Analytics to count visits; see "This Website" below.

Anonymous Analytics Data We Collect

Phlix collects anonymous product-interaction data to understand which features are used, where users encounter problems, and how to improve the app. This data is sent to PostHog (US Cloud). The analytics implementation is a custom, dependency-free client — no third-party analytics SDK is embedded in the app. From version 1.0.18 the App includes RevenueCat's SDK, the only third-party code in the App that sends data to the company that made it; it records purchases (see "Network Communication" below).

What is collected:

  • Which features are used (e.g. timeline viewed, slideshow started, search performed) and where errors occur (e.g. connection failed, image load failed), together with how long those steps took (durations in milliseconds) and the category of each error (an error code or type — never a message that names a file, a server, or an account).
  • App version, build number, iOS version, device model (e.g. "iPhone16,2"), locale and the language the App is showing. Country-level breakdowns use the locale's region.
  • The screen you are on, whether the device is upright or sideways, and the kind of network (Wi-Fi or cellular, never its name), recorded with each event.
  • Crash, hang and app-exit reports from iOS, reduced to error codes, durations, counts and, for a crash, the place in the app's code where it happened (from version 1.0.19).
  • A randomly generated device identifier (UUID) that is not linked to your Apple ID, Plex account, or any personal information. The install record keyed by that identifier also carries the current purchase tier described below.
  • Which Pro option you started or completed, by its product identifier (trial, yearly, lifetime), with the currency of your App Store — never transaction IDs, prices, payment details or your Apple ID. (RevenueCat's purchase records, below, do include transaction IDs and prices.) Versions before 1.0.13 also sent the option's list price.
  • Your device's IP address reaches PostHog with each request, as it does for any internet request, and PostHog can keep it with the event. From version 1.0.13 Phlix turns off PostHog's location lookup, so no country or city is derived from it; earlier versions did not. The developer does not use it to identify anyone.

What is never collected:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or any contact information.
  • Your Plex username, authentication tokens, server name, server address, or any server configuration.
  • Photos, videos, filenames, album names, or any content from your Plex library or from your device's Photo Library.
  • Apple advertising identifiers (IDFA) or cross-app tracking data.
  • Payment information or billing details. App Store transaction records are not part of these analytics; RevenueCat keeps them for the developer (see "Network Communication" below).

You can opt out of analytics entirely in the App under Settings → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage data." When opted out, no analytics data is sent and any queued data is deleted. This setting does not stop the App's contact with RevenueCat (see "Network Communication" below).

The App contains no advertising frameworks, no tracking pixels, and no session-replay tools. No ATT (App Tracking Transparency) prompt is required because no tracking occurs.

Information Stored Locally on Your Device

The following information is stored on your device and is not transmitted to the developer. There is one exception: if you share anonymous usage data, an event can include how an option was set at that moment, for example whether a slideshow was shuffled or whether Camera Upload included videos.

  • Plex Authentication Token — Stored securely in the iOS Keychain. Obtained directly from Plex, Inc. via Plex's official OAuth PIN flow. Used to authenticate API requests to your Plex Media Server.
  • Server Connection Details — The address(es), display name, and identifier of your Plex Media Server, stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • App Preferences — Selected library, slideshow defaults, mobile-data preferences, and similar settings, stored in UserDefaults on your device.
  • Favorites — A list of media-item identifiers you have favorited within the App, stored in a local file on your device.
  • Image and Metadata Cache — Thumbnails, full-resolution images, and library indexes are cached on your device to improve performance. You can clear the image cache at any time in the App's Settings, or by uninstalling the App.
  • Camera Upload Settings (only if you set up Camera Upload) — The address, name and identifier of your Camera Upload helper, and the fingerprint of its certificate. The identifier of the Plex Media Server it runs next to. A pairing token and a random identifier for this device (both in the iOS Keychain). The folder name you chose. Your Camera Upload options. If you share anonymous usage data, it says how Camera Upload was set when you switched it on: with or without videos, with or without screenshots, and to back up everything or only new photos; see "Camera Upload" below. The address range of your home network (for example 192.168.1.0/24, never the Wi-Fi's name). Lists of which items in your Photo Library have been uploaded, cannot be uploaded, or were left for later. The file names of the last 60 uploads, for the list on the Camera Upload screen. Dates and counters, such as when you paired, when the last upload was, and how many free uploads you have used. While a file is being sent, a temporary copy of it waits on your device and is deleted afterwards. Whether or not you set up Camera Upload, from version 1.0.21 the App notes which items in your Photo Library it saved there from your server, so that Camera Upload never sends them back.

Apart from that exception, none of this is transmitted to the developer. Uninstalling the App removes its files and preferences from your device. What is kept in the iOS Keychain can stay on the device after an uninstall: your Plex authentication token, your server connection details and, if you set up Camera Upload, its pairing token and device identifier. To remove them, choose Log Out before you uninstall. If you set up Camera Upload, choose Disconnect on the Camera Upload screen first. The device identifier stays even then. It is a random value. The App sends it only to your own helper, when you pair, so that the helper gives this device the same folder again. Like other app data, this information can be part of your own device backups, and can move to a new device with them.

Network Communication

While running, the App communicates directly with the following third parties. The developer is not party to and does not intercept the App's communication with Plex, your Plex Media Server, your Camera Upload helper or Apple. PostHog and RevenueCat process data for the developer, as Google does for this website.

  • Plex, Inc. (plex.tv) — Used for authentication via Plex's OAuth PIN flow and for discovering Plex Media Servers associated with your Plex account. Communication is between your device and Plex's servers. Plex's handling of your data is governed by Plex's Privacy Policy.
  • Your Plex Media Server — Used to browse, search, stream, and download media stored on that server. Communication is between your device and the server's address (which may be on your local network or reached over the internet, depending on how you configure your Plex Media Server).
  • Your Camera Upload helper (only if you set up Camera Upload) — A program you install on your own server. The App sends it copies of the photos and videos on your device, over your home network and encrypted. Communication is between your device and that program. See "Camera Upload" below.
  • Apple, Inc. — The App is distributed through the Apple App Store; Apple processes purchases and subscriptions, distributes the App, and may collect data about App downloads and use in accordance with Apple's standard policies. Apple's handling of that data is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy. The developer does not receive your name, email address, Apple ID or billing information from Apple. Apple sends records of your purchases, such as renewals and refunds, to RevenueCat for the developer; see RevenueCat below.
  • PostHog, Inc. (us.posthog.com) — Anonymous product-interaction events are sent to PostHog's US Cloud infrastructure for analytics. No names, contact details, Plex credentials, server addresses, or media content are included. See "Anonymous Analytics Data We Collect" above for the full list. You can opt out in the App's Settings. PostHog's handling of data is governed by PostHog's Privacy Policy.
  • RevenueCat, Inc. (api.revenuecat.com, from version 1.0.18) — Keeps a record of your App Store purchases and trials in the App, including ones made before version 1.0.18, for the developer's sales figures. It decides nothing about what you can use. The App contacts RevenueCat each time it starts, whether or not you buy anything. Its requests carry the random identifier described above, the identifier iOS gives this developer's apps on your device (IDFV), your device model, iOS and App versions, preferred languages, App Store country, the App's tracking-permission status (Phlix never asks to track you) and your IP address. For a purchase or trial it also receives the App Store's record of it: product, price, currency, dates and transaction identifiers. It never receives your name, email address, Apple ID, payment details, Plex account or photos. The "Share anonymous usage data" setting does not turn it off. RevenueCat's handling of data is governed by RevenueCat's Privacy Policy.
  • Google LLC (Google Analytics, website only) — Used on the pages of phlixapp.com, except the privacy, terms and support pages, to count visits and clicks on App Store links. The App itself never contacts Google. A link from the App to the setup guide opens that page in your browser. See "This Website" below.

Photo Library Access

If you choose to save downloaded media to your device's Photo Library, the App will request the iOS "Add Photos Only" permission. That permission is used solely to save media you explicitly choose to download. If you choose an album to save into, the App asks for access to your Photo Library so that it can list your albums; it reads their names and nothing else. If you turn on Camera Upload, the App reads the photos and videos in your Photo Library in order to copy them to your own server, as described below. No photo, video, file name or album name from your Photo Library is ever sent to the developer. If you share anonymous usage data, it includes Camera Upload's counts, such as how many items there are to back up; see "Camera Upload" below.

Camera Upload (optional, beta, from version 1.0.21)

Camera Upload is off unless you set it up. It copies the photos and videos in your device's Photo Library to a folder on a server that you run, so that they appear in your Plex library. The Camera Upload setup guide explains how it works and how to install the helper.

  • Where your photos go. To the Camera Upload helper, a program you install on your own server, and nowhere else. They are not sent to the developer, to Plex, Inc., or to any other third party, and the developer cannot see or reach them.
  • Only at home. The App uploads only while your device is on your home network: the Wi-Fi network it was on when you paired it with the helper, or one you add yourself. The App knows that network by its address range (for example 192.168.1.0/24), never by the Wi-Fi's name. On a network with a different address range it does not start an upload, and it does not look for the helper by itself. There are exceptions. An upload that was already waiting when you left home can try to connect from the new network. A network somewhere else can use the same address range as your home, and there the App may try to reach the helper. If the App could not read your network's address range when you paired, it has no range to compare, and it treats any Wi-Fi network as home. In all of these the App sends your photos and its pairing token only to your own helper (see "Encrypted in transit" below). If you tap "This Is My Home Network" on another network, the App tries your helper's address once, and adds that network only if your helper answers. It never uploads over mobile data.
  • Encrypted in transit. The connection to the helper is encrypted. The helper makes its own certificate, and the App sends your photos and its pairing token only to a server that shows the certificate it saw when you paired. If you give the helper a certificate that iOS already trusts, or put it behind your own reverse proxy that has one, the App relies on iOS's check of that certificate instead.
  • Pairing. If the helper runs next to the Plex Media Server the App is connected to, and can pair through Plex, the App asks that server for a short-lived token that works on that server only, and shows it to the helper when you pair. The helper asks Plex whether the token belongs to the server's owner, and does not keep it. If the token is the owner's, your device is paired without a code. If it is not, or if the helper cannot ask Plex, your device is not paired this way. The other way to pair is a code that the helper shows to the server's owner. Your Plex account's own sign-in token is never sent to the helper.
  • What the helper keeps on your server. The files you upload. For each device that has paired: the folder name you chose, a random identifier, the device's name as iOS gives it (for example "iPhone"), how and when it paired, when it last connected, and, while it is paired, a hash of its pairing token (not the token itself). The helper keeps this record after a device is unpaired, so that the device gets the same folder if it pairs again. A record of each file it has received: an identifier, where the file is stored, its size, its kind (photo or video), its date, when it arrived and a checksum of its content. The helper's log also shows each file as it is stored, and the address a device paired from. All of this stays on your server; the developer has no access to it. The helper's source code is public at github.com/jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload.
  • Nothing is deleted. The App copies. It never deletes or changes anything in your Photo Library.
  • Analytics. If you share anonymous usage data, Camera Upload adds counts and outcomes to it: for example that it was switched on and with which options, how many items there are to back up and how many are left, how many a run uploaded or could not upload, and why it paused. Never a file name, a folder name, an address, or the name of a server, a device or a network.
  • Notifications. If you ask to be told when everything is backed up, the App creates that notification on your device. No push-notification service is involved.

To stop, switch Camera Upload off, or choose Disconnect on its screen to remove the pairing from your device. Files already on your server stay there until you delete them.

Children's Privacy

The App is not directed to children under the age of 13. The App does not knowingly collect personal information from children. The App collects no names, contact details, or account information from anyone, and its analytics are not linked to an identifiable person, so no special procedures are required for users of any age. Parents and guardians should be aware that the App requires a Plex account and access to a Plex Media Server, and should ensure that content accessible via that server is appropriate for any child using the App.

Your Choices and Your Rights

Unless you write to the developer (with the support page's form, or by email, including Send Feedback in the App), the developer holds no name, contact details, or account information about you. If you have written, you can ask through the support page for your messages to be deleted. The analytics records are keyed only by the random device identifier described above, so the developer cannot tell which records are yours and has nothing to access, correct, export, or delete on your behalf; opting out in Settings stops further collection and deletes anything still queued on your device. To exercise the equivalent of access, correction, deletion, or portability rights with respect to data stored on your device:

  • Delete all local data: If you set up Camera Upload, choose Disconnect on the Camera Upload screen first. Then choose Log Out, and uninstall the App. iOS removes the App's files, preferences and image caches. Entries in the iOS Keychain can stay after an uninstall, which is why you disconnect and log out first. The random device identifier of Camera Upload stays in the Keychain even then.
  • Clear caches without uninstalling: Open the App, then Settings, and use the cache-clearing controls.
  • Log out: The App's logout flow removes Plex authentication tokens and server connection details from the Keychain.

Data stored on your own Plex Media Server is under your control, not the developer's. Data Plex, Inc. and Apple, Inc. hold about you is governed by their respective policies linked above; please contact them directly to exercise any rights with respect to that data.
