Camera Upload: back up your iPhone photos to your own Plex server

Updated November 22, 2026 · About 10 minutes to set up

Walk in your front door, and the photos you took today start copying to the server in your home. They show up in Plex, and in Phlix, next to everything else. No cloud, no subscription storage, nothing sent to anyone.

Camera Upload has two parts. The Phlix app on your iPhone or iPad reads your camera roll. A small free program on your server, the Camera Upload helper, receives the photos and writes them into a folder of your Plex photo library. The helper is one Docker container, and this page shows three ways to install it.

  • Only at home. Phlix uploads on your home Wi-Fi and nowhere else. Never over mobile data.
  • Only to you. Photos go from your phone straight to your server, encrypted. Nothing goes to Phlix.
  • Only copies. Nothing is ever deleted or changed on your phone.

How it works

1

Install the helper

One Docker container on the machine that runs Plex Media Server. You point it at a folder inside your Plex photo library.

2

Connect your iPhone

In Phlix, open Settings › Camera Upload. Phlix finds the helper on your network and pairs with it.

3

Come home

Whenever your phone is on your home Wi-Fi, new photos copy to your server. Phlix shows what is backed up and tells you when everything is.

The helper has no screens to learn and no account to create. It has one read-only status page, and one command, doctor, that checks the install and says how to fix what is wrong.

What you need

  • Plex Media Server with a photo library, on a computer or NAS that can run Docker: Linux, Unraid, Synology, TrueNAS SCALE, or a Mac or Windows PC with Docker Desktop. Intel, AMD and Arm processors all work.
  • Phlix 1.0.21 or later on an iPhone or iPad. If the App Store does not offer that version yet, it is on its way.
  • Your phone and your server on the same home network.

The helper is free. In the app, Camera Upload is a beta: the first 100 uploads are free, and Phlix Pro uploads the rest. While it is a beta, keep your own backup of anything you cannot replace.

Step 1: Install the helper

Do this once, on the machine that runs Plex Media Server. Pick the way that suits you. All three give the same result.

First, decide on your upload folder: the folder where your phone's photos will go. It must be inside a folder that one of your Plex photo libraries includes, or Plex never shows the photos. If your photo library's folder is /mnt/media/Photos, a good choice is /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads. To see your library's folders, open Plex in a browser, go to Settings › Manage › Libraries, and choose Edit Library on your photo library.

A. One command Quickest

For Linux servers and NAS systems where you can open a terminal.

Paste this into a terminal on your server:

curl -fsSL https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/install.sh | sh

The installer checks Docker, asks for your upload folder, writes the Compose file into a new folder named phlix-camera-upload in your home folder, starts the helper and runs its check. It never uses sudo, never changes Plex and never deletes a photo. You can read the script before you run it, and try it first with --dry-run, which changes nothing:

curl -fsSL https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/install.sh | sh -s -- --dry-run

Run the installer again at any time to update the helper. The same script is in the GitHub repository.

B. Let an AI assistant do it

For anyone who would rather answer questions than edit files.

The file SETUP-WITH-AI.md holds complete instructions written for an AI assistant: how to find your Plex library's folder, install the helper, check it, and fix what goes wrong. The assistant asks before it changes anything, and it is told never to touch Plex itself or to open anything to the internet.

Any assistant that can run commands on your server

That means Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor and similar tools. In a new, empty folder on your server:

curl -fsSLO https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/SETUP-WITH-AI.md

Then start your assistant in that folder and say: Follow SETUP-WITH-AI.md

Claude Code, as a skill

Add the skill once, from a terminal:

claude plugin marketplace add jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload
claude plugin install phlix-camera-upload@phlix

Then start Claude Code on your server and say: Set up Phlix Camera Upload on this server. The same skill helps later, too: ask it to check, update or fix the helper.

A chat assistant, such as ChatGPT or the Claude app

Give it the file SETUP-WITH-AI.md and say: Walk me through this, one step at a time. It tells you each command, you run it on your server and paste back what it printed. In the Claude apps you can also add the setup skill itself: download phlix-camera-upload-setup.zip and upload it under Customize › Skills.

C. By hand, with Docker Compose

For people who like to see every step. Six steps, one line to edit.

  1. Make your upload folder, as the user who owns your photos. The helper writes files as the owner of this folder. 
    mkdir "/mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads"
  2. Make a folder for the helper and go into it. 
    mkdir phlix-camera-upload && cd phlix-camera-upload
  3. Download the Compose file. 
    curl -fsSLO https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/docker-compose.yml
  4. Open docker-compose.yml in a text editor. Find the comment CHANGE THE NEXT LINE. On the line below it, replace /CHANGE/THIS/Photos/Camera Uploads with your upload folder. Save the file.
  5. Start the helper. The first time, Docker downloads the image. 
    docker compose up -d
  6. Ask the helper whether everything is right. 
    docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor

Prefer docker run? The README has the one command that does the same.

Notes for your system

Unraid

Use the template in place of the Compose file.

  1. Make your upload folder inside a share that your Plex photo library includes, for example /mnt/user/Photos/Camera Uploads.
  2. Open the Unraid terminal and download the template: 
    wget -O /boot/config/plugins/dockerMan/templates-user/my-phlix-camera-upload.xml https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload/main/unraid/phlix-camera-upload.xml
  3. On the Docker tab choose Add Container, and pick phlix-camera-upload in the Template list.
  4. Set Photos to your upload folder and choose Apply.

The template runs the helper as Unraid's standard user and group (PUID 99, PGID 100). To open the status page, choose WebUI on the container's icon. If Plex runs in a container, the upload folder must be inside a folder that the Plex container has.

Synology (Container Manager, DSM 7.2 or later)
  1. In File Station, make your upload folder inside the shared folder that your Plex photo library uses.
  2. In File Station, make a folder for the helper, for example docker/phlix-camera-upload.
  3. Open Container Manager, choose Project, then Create. Enter the name phlix-camera-upload and choose the folder from step 2 as the path.
  4. Choose Create docker-compose.yml as the source and paste the contents of docker-compose.yml.
  5. Change the one line to your upload folder's full path, for example /volume1/photo/Camera Uploads. File Station shows the full path under Properties, as Location.
  6. Finish the wizard. Container Manager downloads the image and starts the helper.

If the DSM firewall is on, allow TCP port 7459 under Control Panel › Security › Firewall. To run a command such as doctor, connect over SSH and put sudo in front of it.

TrueNAS SCALE (24.10 or later)
  1. Make your upload folder inside the dataset that your Plex photo library uses.
  2. Go to Apps › Discover Apps, open the menu with three dots, and choose Install via YAML.
  3. Enter the name phlix-camera-upload and paste the contents of docker-compose.yml.
  4. Change the photos line to your upload folder's full path, for example /mnt/tank/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.
  5. Change ./config:/config to a full path, for example /mnt/tank/apps/phlix-camera-upload:/config, and choose Save.

To run a command, open System › Shell and put sudo in front of it.

A Mac or Windows PC with Docker Desktop
  • Plex runs on the Mac or PC itself, not in Docker. The helper still finds it.
  • Write the photos line as a Mac or Windows path: /Users/you/Pictures/Camera Uploads or C:\Users\you\Pictures\Camera Uploads.
  • If Docker says the folder is not shared, add it in Docker Desktop under Settings › Resources › File sharing.
  • If Windows or macOS asks whether Docker may accept connections from your network, allow it.
  • Uploads work only while Docker Desktop runs and the computer is awake. Switch on Settings › General › Start Docker Desktop when you sign in.
  • On Windows, type the commands in PowerShell, and use curl.exe where this page says curl. The one-command installer is for Linux and macOS; on Windows use way B or C.
Plex itself runs in Docker
  • Choose an upload folder that the Plex container can see: it must be inside one of the folders you gave the Plex container.
  • In the helper's Compose file, write the folder's path as it is on the server, not as it is inside the Plex container. If Plex has /mnt/media/Photos:/data/photos, the helper's line is /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.
  • The helper finds Plex on the server's port 32400 by itself. If its status page says "Plex Media Server: Not found", set PLEX_URL: see Networking help.

Step 2: Check that it runs

On another device on your home network, open the helper's status page in a browser. Replace SERVER with your server's address, for example https://192.168.1.10:7459.

https://SERVER:7459

Your browser warns that the connection is not private. That is expected. A home server has no public name that a certificate authority could vouch for, so the helper makes its own certificate, and browsers warn about every certificate made that way. Choose Advanced or Show Details, then continue to the page. Phlix does not rely on the browser's check: it remembers the helper's exact certificate when it pairs.

The page should say Upload folder: Ready and Plex Media Server: Found. If it says something else, the doctor command tells you what to do:

docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor
Phlix Camera Upload 1.0.0
OK       The helper is running on port 7459
OK       The upload folder /photos can be written to.
OK       223.6 GB free on the upload disk.
OK       Plex Media Server found: its owner can pair from Phlix without a code.
OK       1 paired, 37 files stored (33.0 MB).
NOTE     Pairing code: run `python -m phlix_upload code` the same way to see it.
Nothing needs fixing.

A line that starts with PROBLEM is followed by a line that starts with Fix:.

One setting in Plex. Plex shows new files only after it scans. When your Plex account owns the server, Phlix asks Plex for a scan once your photos have arrived. For everyone else in your home, and to be sure, switch on Settings › Library › Scan my library automatically in Plex. It is off by default.

Step 3: Connect your iPhone

  1. Connect your iPhone or iPad to your home Wi-Fi.
  2. Open Phlix and go to Settings › Camera Upload.
  3. Tap Check for the Helper, then Check My Network. Phlix looks for the helper at your Plex server's address. Nothing is switched on, and nothing is uploaded, until it is found.
  4. When Phlix says Helper found, choose a folder name for this device, such as "Anna's iPhone", and tap Connect.
  5. Choose what to back up, Everything on this device or Only new photos, and tap Turn On Camera Upload.
  6. iOS asks whether Phlix may read your photos. Choose Full Access, so that it can back up all of them.

A green dot and "Connected to" your server's name mean the connection is good. Grey means you are away from home, where Phlix waits. Orange means something needs your attention, and the screen says what.

Pairing: who needs a code

  • You own the Plex server. Phlix pairs without a code. The app shows the helper a short-lived token from your Plex server, the helper asks Plex whether it belongs to the server's owner, and then forgets it.
  • Everyone else, such as family members with their own Plex account, types the helper's pairing code. The owner of the server finds it with this command, or in the helper's log each time it starts: 
    docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload code
    Capital letters, spaces and the dash do not matter.

If Phlix says Helper not found, Camera Upload stays off, and nothing is sent anywhere. Read The app does not find the helper.

What happens next

  • With Phlix open, uploads start straight away and run as fast as your Wi-Fi allows. The screen stays awake while the Camera Upload screen is uploading, so a first big backup is quickest with the phone on its charger and that screen open.
  • With Phlix closed, iOS lets it carry on in short bursts, mostly while the phone charges. That is how iOS treats every app, and it means a large library takes a few days in the background. New photos follow by themselves.
  • Away from home, nothing is sent, and Phlix does not even look for your server. Backing up carries on when the phone is back on your home Wi-Fi.
  • When everything is backed up, the screen says so with a green tick. Switch on Notify Me When Backed Up to get a notification as well.

The Camera Upload screen always shows where you stand: how many photos are backed up out of how many, what is uploading now, what was backed up recently, and anything that could not be backed up, with the reason.

What is uploaded

  • The photos and videos in your own Photo Library. Videos are optional (Include Videos).
  • Screenshots and screen recordings are left out unless you switch on Include Screenshots.
  • Each photo is uploaded once, as it looks in Photos at that moment. An edited photo uploads edited. A Live Photo uploads as its still photo.
  • With iCloud Photos and "Optimize iPhone Storage", iOS fetches the full-size original from iCloud when Phlix asks for it.
  • Not uploaded: hidden photos, shared albums, photos synced from a computer, and photos that Phlix itself saved to your library from Plex.

Where your photos go

Each phone or tablet has its own folder inside the upload folder. Inside it there is one folder for each year and one for each month:

Camera Uploads/
  Anna's iPhone/
    2026/
      2026-11/
        IMG_1234.HEIC
        IMG_1235.MOV
  Ben's iPad/
    2026/
      2026-10/
  • The year and month come from the photo's own date. The file keeps its name from the phone, and its modification time is set to the photo's date.
  • Two different photos with the same name are both kept: the second becomes IMG_1234 (2).HEIC. Nothing is ever overwritten.
  • The same photo from two phones is stored once.
  • Files you move or delete on the server are not asked for again.
  • To change the layout, set PATH_TEMPLATE. See Changing things later.

Networking help

Most homes need nothing from this section: the helper runs on the machine that runs Plex, the phone is on the same Wi-Fi, and Phlix finds it. Read on if your network is less ordinary, or if Phlix says Helper not found.

First, one test

On the iPhone, open Safari and go to https://SERVER:7459, with your server's address. Tap through the certificate warning.

  • The status page opens. The network is fine. In Phlix, tap Enter the Helper's Address and type the same address. If Phlix still cannot reach it, check the iPhone's own settings.
  • The page does not open. The phone cannot reach the helper. Find your situation below.

How Phlix finds the helper

Phlix asks nothing of your network except one thing: it must be able to open a connection from the phone to TCP port 7459 on the server. There is no broadcast, no discovery protocol and no cloud relay. Phlix takes the local addresses of the Plex server you are signed in to, tries port 7459 on each, and accepts only an answer from the real helper. If your helper is somewhere else, you type its address once.

The helper is on a different machine than Plex, or on another port

In Phlix, tap Enter the Helper's Address and type it, for example 192.168.1.20, or 192.168.1.20:8443 for another port.

On another machine, two more things matter:

  • The upload folder must still be inside your Plex photo library. Usually that means a network share that both machines mount. The helper writes files as the owner of the folder, or as PUID and PGID.
  • Set PLEX_URL to your Plex server's address, for example http://192.168.1.10:32400, so that the server's owner can pair without a code. Without it, everyone pairs with the pairing code, which works just as well.

To use another port on the same machine, change only the first number in the ports: line of the Compose file, for example "8443:7459".

Plex runs in Docker, and the helper says "Plex Media Server: Not found"

Phones can still pair with the pairing code. Only pairing without a code needs the helper to reach Plex.

The helper looks for Plex on port 32400 at the Docker host, at the container's gateway, at 127.0.0.1 and at a container named plex. That covers Plex on the server itself, Plex in Docker with host networking, and Plex in Docker with port 32400 published. If yours is elsewhere, tell the helper. In docker-compose.yml, remove the # in front of environment: and PLEX_URL, write your Plex server's address, and run docker compose up -d:

    environment:
      PLEX_URL: "http://192.168.1.10:32400"

If Plex runs in Docker with bridge networking and Phlix does not find the helper by itself, Plex is reporting its address inside Docker instead of the server's own. Type the server's address into Phlix once.

A firewall on the server

Allow TCP port 7459 from your home network, and from nowhere else.

  • ufw (Ubuntu, Debian): sudo ufw allow from 192.168.1.0/24 to any port 7459 proto tcp, with your own network range.
  • firewalld (Fedora, RHEL, Rocky): sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-port=7459/tcp, then sudo firewall-cmd --reload.
  • Synology: Control Panel › Security › Firewall.
  • Windows and macOS: allow Docker when the system asks whether it may accept incoming connections.

If the helper cannot find Plex on the same machine, the firewall may also be keeping the container away from port 32400. Set PLEX_URL to the server's own address, or pair with the pairing code.

Guest Wi-Fi, "client isolation", or a separate network for phones or smart devices (VLANs)

Guest networks and routers with "AP isolation" or "client isolation" keep devices from talking to each other. That is their job, and it stops the phone from reaching the server.

  • The simple fix: put the phone on the same network as the server.
  • If you keep phones and servers on separate networks on purpose, add one rule in your router or firewall: allow the phones' network to reach the server's address on TCP port 7459. Phlix needs nothing else, and no discovery traffic has to cross between the networks. Then type the server's address into Phlix.

Mesh Wi-Fi, several access points and a server on a cable are all fine, as long as they are one network.

Phlix says "Paused: not on your home network", but I am at home

Phlix knows your home by the address range of the Wi-Fi network your phone was on when you paired, for example 192.168.1.0/24. It never reads the Wi-Fi's name and needs no location permission. If you changed your router, moved to another network at home, or have two networks, the range no longer matches.

Open Settings › Camera Upload and tap This Is My Home Network. Phlix adds the network you are on, but only if your helper answers from it, with the certificate it had when you paired. That is what keeps a network elsewhere from ever counting as home.

The iPhone's own settings: Local Network, VPNs
  • Local Network. iOS asks once whether Phlix may find devices on your local network. If the answer was no, Phlix cannot reach anything at home. Open the iPhone's Settings › Apps › Phlix (or Privacy & Security › Local Network) and switch on Local Network. Phlix tells you when this is the reason.
  • VPN apps. Many VPN apps block the local network while they are on. Look for a setting named "Allow LAN access" or "Local network sharing", or pause the VPN at home. iCloud Private Relay does not get in the way.
  • Mobile data. Phlix never uploads over mobile data. Low Data Mode on your home Wi-Fi does not hold uploads back: they stay on your own network.
My server's address changes

Give the server a fixed address: in your router, look for "DHCP reservation" or "static lease" and reserve the address the server has now. That helps Plex as much as the helper.

If the address does change, Phlix looks for the helper again at your Plex server's new address by itself, and carries on once it finds the same helper there. You can also disconnect and connect again; nothing is uploaded twice.

Away from home: remote access, VPNs into your home, port forwarding

Camera Upload is made for your home network, on purpose. Phlix checks the Wi-Fi network the phone is on, so a VPN into your home from somewhere else does not start uploads, and neither does Plex Remote Access.

Do not forward port 7459 to the internet. It would add nothing, because Phlix only uploads at home, and it would expose a service on your server to everyone.

A reverse proxy, your own domain name, or your own certificate

You do not need any of these. If you already run them at home and prefer a name such as https://uploads.home.example:

  • Your own certificate: put the certificate and key in the helper's config folder and set TLS_CERT and TLS_KEY. With a certificate that iOS already trusts, Phlix uses iOS's own check, so a renewed certificate needs no new pairing.
  • A reverse proxy (Nginx Proxy Manager, Traefik, Caddy): set TLS: "off" on the helper and let the proxy add HTTPS. Phlix connects only over HTTPS. Lift the proxy's upload size limit and switch off request buffering, or large videos fail: in Nginx, client_max_body_size 0; and proxy_request_buffering off;.
  • In Phlix, type the full address: https://uploads.home.example.
  • Keep it inside your home. Do not route uploads through a cloud proxy: it would see your photos, and most limit uploads to 100 MB.

The home-network rule stays the same: Phlix uploads only on your home Wi-Fi, whatever the address.

Docker network modes: host, macvlan, Unraid's br0
  • Bridge with a published port is what the Compose file uses, and what works everywhere.
  • Host networking works too: replace the ports: and extra_hosts: lines with network_mode: host.
  • macvlan, ipvlan or Unraid's br0 give the helper its own address on your network. Type that address into Phlix. A container on such a network usually cannot reach the server it runs on, so set PLEX_URL to an address it can reach, or pair with the pairing code.
  • Docker inside WSL 2 without Docker Desktop: Windows does not pass connections from your network into WSL by default. Use Docker Desktop, or WSL's mirrored networking mode.

The helper uses IPv4. A home network with only IPv6 is not supported yet.

Troubleshooting

Start with the helper's own check. It tests the install and says how to fix what is wrong.

docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor

If it says that the container is not running, read the log instead: docker logs phlix-camera-upload. In Phlix, the Camera Upload screen names the problem it sees, and Check Connection tries again.

Changing things later

The helper has no settings screen. Everything is one file, docker-compose.yml, and one command to apply it: docker compose up -d, in the helper's folder.

Move the upload folder

Stop the helper with docker compose down, move the folder on the server, change the source: line in docker-compose.yml, and run docker compose up -d. Phones stay paired, and nothing is uploaded twice. Remember that the new place must be inside a Plex photo library too.

Change a device's folder name

A device keeps the folder it chose when it first paired. To use another name, choose Disconnect in Phlix, connect again and type the new name. Photos already uploaded stay in the old folder, and are not uploaded again.

Update the helper 
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d

Run both in the helper's folder. Phones stay paired: the helper keeps its data in the config folder, and an update does not touch it. On Unraid, use Check for Updates on the Docker tab.

Remove the helper 
docker compose down

Removing the helper never deletes photos. Everything it received stays in your upload folder. To remove the helper's own data as well, delete the folder you made for it. In Phlix, switch Camera Upload off or choose Disconnect.

Privacy and security

  • Your photos stay on your network. The phone sends them straight to your server. Phlix, the developer, never receives them and cannot reach them. The helper contacts nothing on the internet; the only thing it calls is your own Plex Media Server.
  • The connection is encrypted. When a phone pairs, Phlix remembers the helper's exact certificate. From then on it talks only to a server that shows it.
  • Pairing needs proof. The owner of the Plex server proves it through Plex, and the helper never stores a Plex token. Everyone else needs the pairing code, and the helper slows down anyone who guesses.
  • A paired phone can only add. The helper offers no way to list, read, download, overwrite or delete a stored file. It accepts photo and video files only.
  • Nothing is deleted on your phone. Phlix copies. It never deletes or changes anything in your Photo Library.
  • You can read the code. The helper's source is public on GitHub, with the security notes.

Questions

Does it delete photos from my iPhone?

No. Phlix copies your photos and videos to your server. It never deletes or changes anything in your Photo Library. Freeing space on the phone stays your decision.

Does it work away from home, or over mobile data?

No, on purpose. Phlix uploads only while the phone is on your home Wi-Fi. Away from home it sends nothing and does not look for your server. Photos you take on a trip upload when you are back.

Do I need Plex Pass?

No. The helper writes ordinary files into a folder of your Plex photo library, and that works with every Plex Media Server. The photos then show in every Plex app, and in Phlix.

What does it cost?

The helper for your server is free. In the app, the first 100 uploads are free, so you can see it work from end to end. Phlix Pro uploads the rest, and every photo you take from then on.

Can the whole family use it?

Yes. Each iPhone and iPad pairs by itself and gets its own folder. The owner of the Plex server pairs without a code; everyone else types the helper's pairing code. If two phones hold the same photo, the server stores it once.

How long does the first backup take?

With Phlix open on the Camera Upload screen and the phone on its charger, it runs as fast as your Wi-Fi allows. With Phlix closed, iOS gives it short bursts, mostly while the phone charges, so a large library takes a few days. Either way you can see the count rise in the app.

Is this a sync? What if I delete a photo?

It is a one-way copy, from the phone to the server. Deleting a photo on the phone leaves the copy on the server. Deleting or moving a file on the server does not bring it back from the phone: each photo is uploaded once.

Does it work with iCloud Photos?

Yes. Phlix reads the photos in your Photo Library, wherever iOS keeps them. With "Optimize iPhone Storage", iOS downloads the full-size original from iCloud when Phlix asks for it, and Phlix needs about 300 MB free on the phone to get each file ready. Shared albums are not included.

Is there an Android version?

No. Phlix is an app for iPhone and iPad.
