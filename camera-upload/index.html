Camera Upload (Beta): back up your iPhone photos to your own Plex server

Updated November 27, 2026 · About 10 minutes to set up

On your iPhone or iPad? Step 1 is done on your server: open phlixapp.com/camera-upload on a computer to copy the commands. Helper installed already? Go to Step 2: Connect your iPhone. Something not working? See Troubleshooting.

Camera Upload backs up the photos and videos on your iPhone or iPad to the server in your home, while the device is on your home Wi-Fi. They show up in Plex, and in Phlix, next to everything else. No cloud and no storage subscription.

Camera Upload is a beta in Phlix 1.0.21 or later: keep your own backup of anything you cannot replace. See what the beta does and what it does not do.

Camera Upload has two parts. The Phlix app on your iPhone or iPad reads your camera roll. A small free program on your server, the Camera Upload helper, receives the photos and writes them into a folder of your Plex photo library. The helper is one Docker container, and this page shows three ways to install it.

Plex had a Camera Upload of its own, and announced in March 2021 that it was ending it. Camera Upload in Phlix is a separate feature of an independent app that works with Plex Media Server. What happened to Plex's Camera Upload, and the other ways to get iPhone photos into Plex.

  • Only at home. Phlix uploads on your home Wi-Fi and nowhere else. Never over mobile data.
  • Only to you. Photos go from your phone straight to your server, encrypted. None of them go to Phlix.
  • Only copies. Nothing is ever deleted or changed on your phone.
  • Free to try. The helper is free, and so are the first 100 uploads. Phlix Pro uploads the rest.

How it works

1

Install the helper

One Docker container on the machine that runs Plex Media Server. You point it at a folder inside your Plex photo library.

2

Connect your iPhone

In Phlix, open Settings › Camera Upload. Phlix finds the helper on your network and pairs with it once: from then on, the helper takes photos only from paired devices, and Phlix talks only to that helper.

3

Back up at home

On your home Wi-Fi, new photos copy to your server: straight away while Phlix is open, and in short bursts while it is closed. Phlix shows what is backed up and tells you when everything is.

What Camera Upload does, and does not do

Camera Upload is a beta in Phlix 1.0.21 or later: it works, and it is new. Keep your own backup of anything you cannot replace, and tell us what goes wrong on the support page.

What it does

  • Backs up the photos and videos in the Photo Library of your iPhone or iPad to a folder of your Plex photo library, on your own server. You choose what to back up: Include Videos (on at first), Include Screenshots (off at first), and Everything on this device or Only new photos.
  • Copies each photo as it looks in Photos at that moment: an edited photo uploads edited, a Live Photo as its still photo. It never deletes or changes anything on the device.
  • Checks before it starts. Phlix looks for the helper at your Plex server's address on port 7459, or at an address you type. If the helper is not found, Camera Upload stays off and Phlix says why.
  • Pairs with proof. The owner of the Plex server pairs without a code; everyone else types the helper's pairing code. The connection is encrypted (HTTPS), and Phlix talks only to the helper whose certificate it saw when it paired: a certificate is the helper's proof of who it is.
  • Uploads only on your home Wi-Fi: the network the device was on when it paired, or one you add with This Is My Home Network. With Phlix open, new photos are picked up and uploaded by themselves.
  • Shows where you stand: a green dot and "Connected to" the helper's name when the connection is good; how many items are backed up out of how many, how many are still to back up, what is uploading now, what was backed up recently, and how many could not be backed up. It says Everything is backed up when it is, and sends a notification too if you switch on Notify Me When Backed Up.
  • Keeps a folder for each device, with a folder for each year and month. A file keeps its name and its date. The same photo from two devices is stored once. Nothing is ever overwritten. When your Plex account owns the server, Phlix asks Plex to scan once your photos have arrived, so they show up in Plex.
  • Needs one free helper: one Docker container, for Intel, AMD and Arm processors, with a read-only status page and a doctor command that checks the install and says how to fix what is wrong. It has no account and no cloud service.
  • The first 100 uploads are free. Phlix Pro uploads the rest.

What it does not do

  • It does not upload away from home, over mobile data, or through a VPN into your home.
  • It is not a sync. Deleting a photo on the phone leaves the copy on the server, and nothing comes back to the phone.
  • It does not upload the video part of a Live Photo, hidden photos, shared albums, or photos synced from a computer.
  • It does not find the helper by itself when the helper is on another machine or another port than Plex. You type the address once.
  • With Phlix closed, it uploads only when iOS allows it: in short bursts, mostly while the device charges. A large library takes a few days in the background.
  • It works on iPhone and iPad only, and on home networks that use IPv4 addresses, such as 192.168.1.10. A network with only IPv6 is not supported.

What you need

  • Plex Media Server with a photo library, on a computer or NAS that runs Docker. Docker is a program that runs other programs in closed boxes called containers. It is free for personal use, and the helper comes as one container. The helper needs Docker 20.10 or later. Intel, AMD and Arm processors all work. It runs on Linux, on a Mac or Windows PC with Docker Desktop, and on Unraid, Synology (some models), TrueNAS SCALE and QNAP.
    Docker on your system: what to install first, and what to check
    • Linux: install Docker with Docker's own instructions, not from a Snap: Docker from a Snap, which Ubuntu Server offers while it installs, cannot start the helper. If snap list docker lists docker, The helper does not start says how to replace it. You also need Docker Compose version 2, the docker compose command: the part of Docker that starts a program from one small text file. If docker compose version does not answer, add it. If you installed Ubuntu's own package with sudo apt install docker.io, add Ubuntu's Compose package instead: sudo apt install docker-compose-v2 (in Ubuntu 22.04 and later).
    • Mac or Windows PC: install Docker Desktop and start it. Docker Compose comes with it. The helper runs only while Docker Desktop runs.
    • Synology: install Container Manager from Package Center. It needs DSM 7.2 or later, and Synology offers it only on some models: see the Synology steps.
    • Unraid: Docker is part of Unraid. You do not need Docker Compose there.
    • TrueNAS SCALE 24.10 or later: Docker is part of it.
    • QNAP: install Container Station from App Center. The steps on this page are for Container Station 3, which needs QTS 5.1 or QuTS hero h5.1 or later.
  • Phlix 1.0.21 or later on an iPhone or iPad.
  • Your phone and your server on the same home network.

The helper is free. In the app, the first 100 uploads are free, and Phlix Pro uploads the rest.

Step 1: Install the helper

Reading this on your phone? Step 1 is done on your server. Open phlixapp.com/camera-upload on a computer, so that you can copy the commands.

Do this once, on the machine that runs Plex Media Server. First, decide on your upload folder: the folder where your phone's photos will go. It must be inside a folder that one of your Plex photo libraries includes, or Plex never shows the photos. To see your library's folders, open Plex in a browser, go to Settings › Manage › Libraries, choose Edit Library on your photo library, then Add folders. If the folder there is /mnt/media/Photos, a good choice is /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.

Does your Plex run in Docker itself? That is usual on Unraid and TrueNAS SCALE. Plex then shows the folder's path inside its own container, such as /data/Photos. The helper needs the folder's path on the server, such as /mnt/media/Photos. Read Plex itself runs in Docker before you go on.

Then find your system. It decides which steps you follow.

  • A Linux computer or server: way A, B or C below.
  • A Mac with Docker Desktop: way A, B or C below. Read the note for Mac and Windows first.
  • A Windows PC with Docker Desktop: way B or C below. Read the note for Mac and Windows first. Way A is for Linux and macOS.
  • Unraid: the Unraid steps, or way B. Unraid has no Docker Compose, so ways A and C do not work there.
  • Synology: the Synology steps, which use Container Manager in place of the command line, or way B.
  • TrueNAS SCALE: the TrueNAS SCALE steps, or way B. Do not use way A there: let TrueNAS manage the helper.
  • QNAP: the QNAP steps, which use Container Station in place of the command line. Way B's instructions do not cover QNAP.
New to typing commands? Where to type them

The commands on this page are typed into a terminal: a window where you type a line and press Return. Type them on your server, the machine that runs Plex Media Server.

  • Linux with a screen: open the terminal program. It is often named Terminal.
  • Linux without a screen: sign in from another computer with SSH, a way to type commands on one computer from another. In a terminal on the other computer, type ssh NAME@SERVER, with your user name on the server and the server's address.
  • Mac: open the app named Terminal. It is in Applications › Utilities.
  • Windows: open PowerShell.
  • Unraid, Synology, TrueNAS SCALE and QNAP: read Steps and notes for your system, below, first. It says where to type commands on each, and the install steps differ there.

A command in a box has a Copy button. Paste the command into the terminal and press Return.

If Docker answers "permission denied", put sudo in front of each docker command. sudo runs one command as the administrator and may ask for your password. If the one-command installer needs this, it stops and shows the command to use.

A. Run one command Quickest

For a Linux computer or server, and for a Mac with Docker Desktop. Not for Windows, Unraid, TrueNAS SCALE or QNAP: find your system in the list above.

Paste this into a terminal on your server:

curl -fsSL https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/install.sh | sh

The installer checks Docker and asks for your upload folder. It writes the Compose file, docker-compose.yml, into a new folder named phlix-camera-upload in your home folder. The Compose file is a small text file that tells Docker how to run the helper. Then the installer starts the helper and runs its check. When all is well, it prints Phlix Camera Upload is running. and, under that, a line that starts with Status page: with the address of the helper's status page.

It never uses sudo, never changes Plex and never deletes a photo. You can read the script before you run it (if your browser saves it instead of showing it, read it on GitHub), and try it first with --dry-run, which changes nothing:

curl -fsSL https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/install.sh | sh -s -- --dry-run
Install without questions, or update later

To answer the installer's questions ahead of time, for example where there is no terminal to ask on, give it your upload folder with --photos and add --yes. It then asks nothing, and it makes the folder if it is missing. Put your own upload folder in place of the example:

curl -fsSL https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/install.sh | sh -s -- --photos "/mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads" --yes

Run the installer again at any time to update the helper. If you installed with --photos and --yes, leave both out when you update: the installer then keeps your Compose file as it is. With them, it can write a new Compose file that keeps only your upload folder, port, PLEX_URL, PUID and PGID. The old file stays next to the new one.

Done when the installer prints Phlix Camera Upload is running. Next: check that the helper runs.

B. Let an AI assistant do it

For anyone who would rather answer questions than edit files.

The file SETUP-WITH-AI.md holds complete instructions written for an AI assistant: how to find your Plex library's folder, install the helper, check it, and fix what goes wrong. The assistant asks before it changes anything outside the helper's own folder, and it is told never to touch Plex itself or to open anything to the internet.

Any assistant that can run commands on your server

Such as Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI or Cursor. Download the instructions into a new, empty folder on your server:

curl -fsSLO https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/SETUP-WITH-AI.md

Then start your assistant in that folder and say: Follow SETUP-WITH-AI.md

Claude Code, as a skill

Add the skill once, from a terminal:

claude plugin marketplace add jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload
claude plugin install phlix-camera-upload@phlix

Then start Claude Code on your server and say: Set up Phlix Camera Upload on this server. The same skill helps later, too: ask it to check, update or fix the helper.

A chat assistant, such as ChatGPT or the Claude app

Give it the file SETUP-WITH-AI.md and say: Walk me through this, one step at a time. It tells you each command, you run it on your server and paste back what it printed. In the Claude apps you can also add the setup skill itself: download phlix-camera-upload-setup.zip and upload it under Customize › Skills.

Done when the assistant says Phlix Camera Upload is running. and gives you the status page's address. Next: check that the helper runs.

C. Do it by hand, with Docker Compose

For people who like to see every step, on Linux, a Mac or a Windows PC. Six steps, one line to edit. Unraid, Synology, TrueNAS SCALE and QNAP have their own steps below.

  1. Make your upload folder, as the user who owns your photos: the helper writes files as the owner of this folder. Put your own path in place of the example: 
    mkdir "/mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads"
    On Linux, if mkdir says "Permission denied", your user may not write to your library's folder, for example because it belongs to root, or to plex, the user that runs Plex Media Server. Make the folder with sudo, and give it the owner of the folder above it. Put your own paths in place of the examples: 
    sudo mkdir "/mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads"
sudo chown --reference="/mnt/media/Photos" "/mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads"
  2. Make a folder for the helper and go into it. 
    mkdir phlix-camera-upload
cd phlix-camera-upload
  3. Download the Compose file, docker-compose.yml: a small text file that tells Docker how to run the helper. 
    curl -fsSLO https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/docker-compose.yml
  4. Open docker-compose.yml in a text editor. Find the line source: /CHANGE/THIS/Photos/Camera Uploads. It is just below the comment CHANGE THE NEXT LINE. Replace /CHANGE/THIS/Photos/Camera Uploads with your upload folder. Save the file. If you would rather not edit a file, use way A or B: each writes this file for you.
  5. Start the helper. The first time, Docker downloads the image: the helper, packed for Docker. 
    docker compose up -d
  6. Ask the helper whether everything is right. 
    docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor
The Compose file, exactly as you download it

Change only the line under CHANGE THE NEXT LINE.

# Phlix Camera Upload
#
# This file starts the helper that receives the photos and videos the Phlix
# app uploads from your iPhone or iPad, and writes them into a folder of your
# Plex photo library.
#
# How to use this file:
#   1. Change the one line marked CHANGE THE NEXT LINE below.
#   2. In the folder that holds this file, run:  docker compose up -d
#   3. Open https://YOUR-SERVER:7459 in a browser to check that it runs.
#      The browser warns about the certificate. That is expected.
#
# If something does not work, ask the helper what is wrong:
#   docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor
#
# Setup guide: https://phlixapp.com/camera-upload/
# If the app cannot find the helper:
#   https://github.com/jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload/blob/main/NETWORKING.md

services:
  phlix-camera-upload:
    image: ghcr.io/jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload:1
    container_name: phlix-camera-upload

    # Start again by itself when the server or Docker restarts.
    restart: unless-stopped

    # The Phlix app looks for the helper on port 7459 of your server.
    ports:
      - "7459:7459"

    volumes:
      # Your upload folder: the folder on this server where the photos go.
      #
      # It must be inside a folder that one of your Plex photo libraries
      # includes. If it is not, Plex never shows the photos.
      #
      # Make the folder before you start the helper. Docker refuses to start
      # the helper if the folder is missing. The helper writes files as the
      # user who owns the folder.
      #
      # Write the path as it is on the server itself. If Plex runs in Docker,
      # do not use the path from inside the Plex container.
      #
      # Examples:
      #   Linux      /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads
      #   Synology   /volume1/photo/Camera Uploads
      #   TrueNAS    /mnt/tank/media/Photos/Camera Uploads
      #   Mac        /Users/you/Pictures/Camera Uploads
      #   Windows    C:\Users\you\Pictures\Camera Uploads
      #
      # Spaces in the path are fine. If the path has a "#" after a space, or
      # a ":" before a space, put the whole path in 'single quotes'.
      - type: bind
        # CHANGE THE NEXT LINE. Keep "source: " and write your folder after it.
        source: /CHANGE/THIS/Photos/Camera Uploads
        target: /photos
        bind:
          create_host_path: false

      # The helper's own data: the list of paired phones, the list of files
      # it has received, and its certificate. No photos are kept here.
      # Docker makes a folder named "config" next to this file. Keep it: if
      # it is lost, every phone has to pair again.
      # If your tool has no "next to this file" (TrueNAS, Portainer), write a
      # full path in place of ./config, for example:
      #   /mnt/tank/apps/phlix-camera-upload:/config
      - ./config:/config

    # Lets the helper find Plex Media Server on this machine.
    extra_hosts:
      - "host.docker.internal:host-gateway"

    # The helper can never gain more rights than it was started with.
    security_opt:
      - "no-new-privileges:true"

    # Optional settings. The helper works without any of them.
    #
    # To use one, remove the "# " in front of "environment:" and in front of
    # the setting you want. Leave the other spaces as they are. Then run
    # "docker compose up -d" again.
    #
    # environment:
    #   # Where Plex Media Server is. Set this if the helper's status page
    #   # says "Plex Media Server: Not found", or to make sure that the helper
    #   # asks your own server and no other.
    #   PLEX_URL: "http://192.168.1.10:32400"
    #
    #   # Write files as this user and group. Without these, the helper uses
    #   # the owner of the upload folder.
    #   PUID: "1000"
    #   PGID: "1000"
    #
    #   # Permissions for new files and folders. With 022, other users may
    #   # read them. With 002, the group may also change them.
    #   UMASK: "022"
    #
    #   # The name that the app and the status page show for this helper.
    #   NAME: "Phlix Camera Upload"
    #
    #   # Who may pair. auto: the owner of the Plex server pairs without a
    #   # code and everyone else types the pairing code. code: everyone types
    #   # the code. plex: only the owner of the Plex server may pair.
    #   PAIRING: "auto"
    #
    #   # Your own pairing code, in place of the one the helper makes. Write
    #   # at least 6 letters or digits between the quotes.
    #   PAIRING_CODE: ""
    #
    #   # The folders the helper makes inside the upload folder. You can use
    #   # {device}, {year}, {month} and {day}.
    #   PATH_TEMPLATE: "{device}/{year}/{year}-{month}"
    #
    #   # Refuse uploads that would leave less than this many GB free. Uploads
    #   # that are still arriving count too.
    #   MIN_FREE_GB: "2"
    #
    #   # The largest single file the helper accepts, in GB.
    #   MAX_UPLOAD_GB: "50"
    #
    #   # More file types to accept, besides the usual photo and video types.
    #   # Separate them with commas.
    #   EXTRA_EXTENSIONS: "insp,lrv"
    #
    #   # How much the log says: debug, info, warning or error.
    #   LOG_LEVEL: "info"
    #
    #   # Write "off" only if your own reverse proxy adds HTTPS in front of
    #   # the helper. Phlix talks HTTPS only: with "off" and no such proxy,
    #   # the app cannot use the helper.
    #   TLS: "on"
    #
    #   # Your own certificate and its key, as paths inside the container.
    #   # Set both. A file you put in the config folder is at /config/...
    #   TLS_CERT: "/config/my-cert.pem"
    #   TLS_KEY: "/config/my-key.pem"
    #
    # PORT, BIND, UPLOAD_DIR and CONFIG_DIR exist too. With Docker you do not
    # need them. To use another port on your server, change only the first
    # number in the "ports" line above, for example "8443:7459". The app
    # looks for port 7459 by itself, so with another port you type the
    # helper's address into the app.

Prefer docker run? The README has the one command that does the same.

Done when doctor ends with Nothing needs fixing. Next: check that the helper runs.

Steps and notes for your system

On Unraid, Synology, TrueNAS SCALE and QNAP, follow these steps in place of ways A and C. The notes on Docker Desktop and on Plex in Docker add to the ways above.

Unraid

Unraid has no Docker Compose, so the one-command installer does not work here. Use the template in place of the Compose file.

  1. Open the Unraid terminal.
  2. Make your upload folder inside a share that your Plex photo library includes, and give it to Unraid's standard user and group. Put your own path in place of the example. If the folder is there already, run only the second line. 
    mkdir "/mnt/user/Photos/Camera Uploads"
chown 99:100 "/mnt/user/Photos/Camera Uploads"
  3. Download the template: 
    wget -O /boot/config/plugins/dockerMan/templates-user/my-phlix-camera-upload.xml https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jrl8085/phlix-camera-upload/main/unraid/phlix-camera-upload.xml
  4. On the Docker tab choose Add Container, and pick phlix-camera-upload in the Template list.
  5. Set Photos to your upload folder and choose Apply.

The template runs the helper as Unraid's standard user and group (PUID 99, PGID 100). The chown command above lets that user write to the upload folder. To open the status page, choose WebUI on the container's icon. If the status page says "The helper cannot write here", run the chown command on the folder that you set as Photos, then reload the page. If Plex runs in a container, the upload folder must be inside a folder that the Plex container has.

Next: check that the helper runs.

Synology (Container Manager, DSM 7.2 or later)

Synology offers Container Manager only on some models, and only with DSM 7.2 or later. Synology lists the models under Applied Models on its Container Manager page. If your model is not there, these steps do not work on your NAS. Run the helper on another computer at home instead, as in The helper is on a different machine than Plex, or on another port.

  1. In File Station, make your upload folder inside the shared folder that your Plex photo library uses.
  2. In File Station, make a folder for the helper, for example docker/phlix-camera-upload.
  3. Open Container Manager, choose Project, then Create. Enter the name phlix-camera-upload and choose the folder from step 2 as the path.
  4. Choose Create docker-compose.yml as the source and paste the contents of docker-compose.yml (the full text, with a Copy button).
  5. Change the one line to your upload folder's full path, for example /volume1/photo/Camera Uploads. File Station shows the full path under Properties, as Location.
  6. Finish the wizard. Container Manager downloads the image and starts the helper.

If the DSM firewall is on, allow TCP port 7459 under Control Panel › Security › Firewall.

To run a command such as doctor, switch on SSH first: under Control Panel › Terminal & SNMP › Terminal, tick Enable SSH service and choose Apply. Then sign in with ssh NAME@SERVER, as a DSM user in the administrators group, and put sudo in front of the command. Switch SSH off again when you no longer need it.

To see the pairing code without SSH, open Container Manager, choose Container, select phlix-camera-upload, then choose Action › Details and open the Log tab. Search it for Pairing code.

If the status page says "The helper cannot write here", open Control Panel › Shared Folder › Edit › Permissions and give Read/Write permission to the user who owns the upload folder. Change this in DSM, not with chown or chmod: DSM manages access with its own rules.

Next: check that the helper runs.

TrueNAS SCALE (24.10 or later)

Do not use the one-command installer here: let TrueNAS manage the helper.

  1. Make your upload folder inside the dataset that your Plex photo library uses.
  2. Go to Apps › Discover Apps, open the menu with three dots, and choose Install via YAML.
  3. Enter the name phlix-camera-upload and paste the contents of docker-compose.yml (the full text, with a Copy button).
  4. Change the photos line to your upload folder's full path, for example /mnt/tank/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.
  5. Change ./config:/config to a full path, for example /mnt/tank/apps/phlix-camera-upload:/config, and choose Save.

To run a command, open System › Shell and put sudo in front of it.

Next: check that the helper runs.

QNAP (Container Station 3, QTS 5.1 or QuTS hero h5.1 or later)

Do not use the one-command installer here: let Container Station manage the helper.

  1. In App Center, install Container Station and open it.
  2. In File Station, make your upload folder inside the shared folder that your Plex photo library uses. Its full path starts with /share/ and the name of the shared folder, for example /share/Multimedia/Photos/Camera Uploads. If Plex shows your library's folder as /share/CACHEDEV1_DATA/Multimedia/Photos or similar, that is the same folder, and that path works too.
  3. In File Station, also make a folder for the helper's own data, for example phlix-camera-upload in the shared folder named Container. Its full path is then /share/Container/phlix-camera-upload.
  4. In Container Station, click the arrow next to Explore, at the top, and choose Create Application. Or go to Applications and choose Create.
  5. Enter the name phlix-camera-upload and paste the contents of docker-compose.yml (the full text, with a Copy button).
  6. Change the one line to your upload folder's full path. Change ./config:/config to the folder from step 3, for example /share/Container/phlix-camera-upload:/config. Both paths start with /share/.
  7. Choose Validate, then Create. Container Station downloads the image and starts the helper.

The helper writes files as the owner of the upload folder. On QNAP, the account named admin is the superuser, root. If you made the upload folder as admin, the files belong to admin, and doctor says "The helper runs as root". That is fine: Plex can still read them. To have the files belong to another user, make the upload folder while you are signed in to QTS as that user. The folder then usually belongs to that user, and so do the photos.

To run a command such as doctor, open Containers in Container Station, select phlix-camera-upload and choose Execute. Add the command without docker exec phlix-camera-upload in front, for example python -m phlix_upload doctor, and run it. The pairing code is also on the container's Logs tab: look for Pairing code:.

Where this page says to change docker-compose.yml and run docker compose up -d, open Applications in Container Station instead. Select phlix-camera-upload, click the arrow next to Edit and choose Recreate. Change the text there and choose Update.

If QuFirewall is on and Phlix does not find the helper, open QuFirewall › Firewall Profiles and edit the profile in use. Choose Add Rule. Set Permission to Allow and Source to IP, with your home network's subnet, for example 192.168.1.0/24. Set Protocol to TCP and Port to 7459, and choose Apply. Put the rule above any rule that denies all, then choose Apply again and Yes.

Next: check that the helper runs.

A Mac or Windows PC with Docker Desktop
  • On a Mac, all three ways work. On Windows, use way B or C: the one-command installer is for Linux and macOS.
  • Plex runs on the Mac or PC itself, not in Docker. The helper still finds it.
  • Write the photos line as a Mac or Windows path: /Users/you/Pictures/Camera Uploads or C:\Users\you\Pictures\Camera Uploads.
  • If Docker says the folder is not shared, add it in Docker Desktop under Settings › Resources › File sharing.
  • On Windows, set your home network to Private before you start the helper. Windows 11 makes a network Public when you first connect to it, and the answer below lets the phone in on a Private network only. Choose Private only for your own home network: it lets the other devices there find the PC.
    • Windows 11: open Settings › Network & internet, choose Wi-Fi and then the network you are connected to, or choose Ethernet. Under Network profile type, choose Private network.
    • Windows 10: open Settings › Network & Internet, choose Wi-Fi or Ethernet and then your network. Under Network profile, choose Private.
  • When Windows asks about Docker Desktop Backend, usually the first time the helper starts, make sure that only the box for Private networks is ticked, and choose Allow access. Docker Desktop Backend is the part of Docker Desktop that takes the phone's connections.
  • Windows keeps that answer. If you chose Cancel, closed the window, or allowed it on public networks only, the phone cannot reach the helper on your home network. To be asked again, open the Start menu, type Windows PowerShell, and choose Run as administrator. Run the command below. Then quit Docker Desktop, start it again, and answer as above. 
    Remove-NetFirewallRule -DisplayName "Docker Desktop Backend"
  • On a Mac, if macOS asks whether Docker may accept connections from your network, allow it.
  • Uploads work only while Docker Desktop runs and the computer is awake. Switch on Settings › General › Start Docker Desktop when you sign in.
  • On Windows, type the commands in PowerShell, and use curl.exe where this page says curl.

Then install with way A (on a Mac), way B or way C, above.

Plex itself runs in Docker
  • Choose an upload folder that the Plex container can see: it must be inside one of the folders you gave the Plex container.
  • In the helper's Compose file, write the folder's path as it is on the server, not as it is inside the Plex container. If Plex has /mnt/media:/data and shows your library's folder as /data/Photos, the helper's line is /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.
  • The helper finds Plex on the server's port 32400 by itself. If its status page says "Plex Media Server: Not found", set PLEX_URL: see Networking help.

To see both paths side by side, run this in a terminal on the server, with the name of your Plex container in place of plex:

docker inspect --format '{{range .Mounts}}{{.Type}}  {{.Source}}  =>  {{.Destination}}{{println}}{{end}}' plex

Each line shows a path on the server on the left, and the same folder's path inside Plex on the right. Example: the line is bind /mnt/media => /data, and Plex shows /data/Photos. Your library's folder on the server is then /mnt/media/Photos, and your upload folder is /mnt/media/Photos/Camera Uploads.

To see the name of your Plex container, run:

docker ps --format '{{.Names}}  {{.Image}}'

Then go back to find your system.

Finish Step 1: check that the helper runs

Open the helper's status page in a browser on another device on your home network. The iPhone you will connect is the best one for this: if the page opens in Safari there, the network between the two is fine. Replace SERVER with your server's address, for example https://192.168.1.10:7459.

https://SERVER:7459

Your server's address is four numbers with dots between them, such as 192.168.1.10. To find it:

  • If you used way A, the installer printed it at the end, in the line that starts with Status page:.
  • On Unraid you do not need it here: choose WebUI on the container's icon.
  • On Linux, type hostname -I in the terminal. The address is usually the first one it prints.
  • On a Mac, type ipconfig getifaddr en0 in Terminal. If it prints nothing, try en1 in place of en0.
  • On Windows, type ipconfig in PowerShell and read the line IPv4 Address under Wi-Fi or Ethernet.
  • If you open your NAS in a browser by a name on your home network, such as nas.local, the name works here too: https://nas.local:7459.

Your browser warns that the connection is not private. That is expected: choose Advanced or Show Details, then continue to the page. A home server has no public name that a certificate authority could vouch for, so the helper makes its own certificate, and browsers warn about every certificate made that way. Phlix does not rely on the browser's check: it remembers the helper's exact certificate when it pairs.

The page should say Upload folder: Ready and Plex Media Server: Found. If it says something else, run the doctor command on the server: it tells you what to do. On Synology, TrueNAS SCALE and QNAP, the steps for your system say where to run it.

docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload doctor

One setting in Plex. In Plex, switch on Settings › Library › Scan my library automatically. Plex shows new files only after it scans. Phlix asks Plex for a scan once your photos have arrived, but only when your Plex account owns the server; the setting covers everyone else in your home, and is a second check for you. It depends on the server noticing new files, which typically fails for folders mounted over the network: for those, also switch on Scan my library periodically (Plex: Library settings).

Step 2: Connect your iPhone

  1. Connect your iPhone or iPad to your home Wi-Fi.
  2. Open Phlix and go to Settings › Camera Upload. If Settings has no Camera Upload row, update Phlix: yours is older than 1.0.21. The Version row in Settings shows the number.

    docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload code

Pairing: who needs a code

  • You own the Plex server. Phlix pairs without a code, as long as the helper's status page says Plex Media Server: Found. The app shows the helper a short-lived token from your Plex server, the helper asks Plex whether it belongs to the server's owner, and then forgets it.
  • Everyone else, such as family members with their own Plex account, types the helper's pairing code. The owner of the server finds it with this command, or in the helper's log each time it starts: 
    docker exec phlix-camera-upload python -m phlix_upload code
    Capital letters, spaces and the dash do not matter. On Synology and QNAP, the container's log shows the code without a terminal: see the Synology steps or the QNAP steps.

If Phlix says Helper not found, Camera Upload stays off, and nothing is sent anywhere. Read The app does not find the helper.

Step 2 is done when the Camera Upload screen shows a green dot and "Connected to" the helper's name. To watch the first photos arrive, reload the helper's status page: Files stored goes up. They land in this device's folder inside your upload folder (see Where your photos go), and show in Plex once it has scanned (One setting in Plex). Next: What happens next.

What happens next

  • With Phlix open, uploads start straight away and run as fast as your Wi-Fi allows. While the Camera Upload screen is open and uploading, the phone does not lock by itself, so a first big backup is quickest with the phone on its charger and that screen open.
  • With Phlix closed, iOS lets it carry on in short bursts, mostly while the phone charges. That is how iOS treats every app, and it means a large library takes a few days in the background. New photos follow by themselves.
  • Away from home, Phlix does not start uploads or look for your server, apart from rare cases that the privacy policy lists; even then, your photos go only to your own helper. Backing up carries on when the phone is back on your home Wi-Fi.
  • When everything is backed up, the screen says so with a green tick. Switch on Notify Me When Backed Up to get a notification as well.

The Camera Upload screen always shows where you stand: how many photos are backed up out of how many, what is uploading now, what was backed up recently, and how many items cannot be backed up. Those are items that Photos holds no file for, that are larger than your server accepts, or that keep disappearing from your server.

What is uploaded

  • The photos and videos in your own Photo Library. Videos are optional (Include Videos).
  • Screenshots and screen recordings are left out unless you switch on Include Screenshots.
  • Each photo is uploaded as it looks in Photos at that moment. An edited photo uploads edited. A Live Photo uploads as its still photo.
  • With iCloud Photos and "Optimize iPhone Storage", iOS fetches the full-size original from iCloud when Phlix asks for it.
  • Not uploaded: hidden photos, shared albums and photos synced from a computer.
  • Photos that Phlix 1.0.21 or later saves from Plex to Photos are not uploaded by the phone or tablet that saved them: they came from your server, so they count as backed up without the helper being asked. Phlix keeps the list of them in the app, on that device. Photos saved by an earlier version of Phlix, or before you deleted Phlix and installed it again, are not on the list, and are uploaded like any other photo.

Where your photos go

Each phone or tablet has its own folder inside the upload folder. Inside it there is one folder for each year and one for each month:

Camera Uploads/
  Anna's iPhone/
    2026/
      2026-11/
        IMG_1234.HEIC
        IMG_1235.MOV
  Ben's iPad/
    2026/
      2026-10/
  • The year and month come from the photo's own date. The file keeps its name from the phone, and its modification time is set to the photo's date.
  • Two different photos with the same name are both kept: the second becomes IMG_1234 (2).HEIC. Nothing is ever overwritten.
  • The same photo from two phones is stored once.
  • With helper 1.0.2 or later (see Update the helper), a photo counts as backed up only while its file is in the upload folder. If you delete a file, or a device's whole folder, and the photo is still on the device, Phlix uploads it again. Phlix asks the helper which files are there each time you open the Camera Upload screen, every half minute while that screen is open, at once when you tap Check Connection, and from time to time while it backs up at home. After you connect a device again, nothing that device uploaded counts as backed up until the helper has confirmed it: the screen says Checking… for a moment, then shows the count.
  • To keep a photo off the server, hide it in the Photos app: Phlix never uploads hidden photos.
  • To change the layout, set PATH_TEMPLATE. See All the settings.
If you, or a program, move files on the server
  • Sort files into any folders inside the upload folder, keeping their names: Phlix finds them there. A device's folder you rename is followed, and new photos go into it. A file you rename, or move out of the upload folder, is uploaded again. A file you edit still counts.
  • If a program on the server moves new files away, set it to leave the folders in place. A file that keeps leaving the upload folder soon after each upload is sent again three times at most, then listed under Can't Be Backed Up. A photo whose folder you deleted is never listed there: Phlix sends it again each time (helper 1.0.3 or later). So if the program also removes the folders it empties, the three-times limit never applies, and Phlix uploads the same photos again each time it backs up. When you connect the phone again, or delete the folder that held a listed photo, Phlix sends that photo again too.
  • A recycle bin is not the upload folder. If your upload folder is a whole drive on Windows, a folder you delete goes to that drive's $RECYCLE.BIN. Helper 1.0.3 or later never counts a photo there as backed up; helper 1.0.2 could.

Networking help

Most homes need nothing from this section: the helper runs on the machine that runs Plex, the phone is on the same Wi-Fi, and Phlix finds it. Read on if your network is less ordinary, or if Phlix says Helper not found.

First, test from the iPhone

On the iPhone, open Safari and go to https://SERVER:7459, with your server's address. Tap through the certificate warning.

  • The status page opens. The network is fine. In Phlix, type the same address into the field under Helper not found and tap Check This Address. If Phlix still cannot reach it, check the iPhone's own settings.
  • The page does not open. The phone cannot reach the helper. Find your situation below.

How Phlix finds the helper

Phlix asks nothing of your network except one thing: it must be able to open a connection from the phone to TCP port 7459 on the server. There is no broadcast, no discovery protocol and no cloud relay. Phlix takes the local addresses of the Plex server you are signed in to, tries port 7459 on each, and accepts only an answer from the real helper. If your helper is somewhere else, you type its address once.

The helper's networking guide on GitHub goes further: for each way to set up the network, it says when to use it, which lines to change in docker-compose.yml and what to type into Phlix, with complete examples for nginx and Caddy.

The helper is on a different machine than Plex, or on another port

In Phlix, type the helper's address into the field under Helper not found, for example 192.168.1.20, or 192.168.1.20:8443 for another port, and tap Check This Address. Before a search, tap Enter the Helper's Address to open the field.

On another machine, two more things matter:

  • The upload folder must still be inside your Plex photo library. Usually that means a network share that both machines mount. The helper writes files as the owner of the folder, or as PUID and PGID. Plex may not notice new files on a share by itself. In Plex, also switch on Settings › Library › Scan my library periodically (Plex: Library settings).
  • Set PLEX_URL to your Plex server's address, for example http://192.168.1.10:32400, so that the server's owner can pair without a code. Without it, everyone pairs with the pairing code, which works just as well.

To use another port on the same machine, change only the first number in the ports: line of the Compose file, for example "8443:7459".

A phone that is already paired does not find the helper at a new port, or on another machine than Plex, by itself. It says Can't reach the helper. On that phone, choose Disconnect, tap Check for the Helper, then Enter the Helper's Address, type the new address and tap Check This Address. If the helper still has its config folder, leave the folder name as Phlix shows it: the phone keeps its folder, and nothing is uploaded twice. Phlix asks again what to back up, with the choices you made before already set.

Plex runs in Docker, and the helper says "Plex Media Server: Not found"

Phones can still pair with the pairing code. Only pairing without a code needs the helper to reach Plex.

The helper looks for Plex on port 32400 at the Docker host, at the container's gateway, at 127.0.0.1 and at a container named plex. That covers Plex on the server itself, Plex in Docker with host networking, and Plex in Docker with port 32400 published. If yours is elsewhere, tell the helper. In docker-compose.yml, remove the # in front of environment: and PLEX_URL, write your Plex server's address, and run docker compose up -d:

    environment:
      PLEX_URL: "http://192.168.1.10:32400"

If port 32400 of Plex is not published on the server, the server's address does not answer either. The networking guide on GitHub shows how to put the helper on the Docker network of Plex. Look there for "Plex in a container on its own Docker network".

If Plex runs in Docker with bridge networking and Phlix does not find the helper by itself, Plex is reporting its address inside Docker instead of the server's own. Type the server's address into Phlix once.

A firewall on the server

Allow TCP port 7459 from your home network, and from nowhere else.

  • ufw (Ubuntu, Debian): sudo ufw allow from 192.168.1.0/24 to any port 7459 proto tcp, with your own network range.
  • firewalld (Fedora, RHEL, Rocky): sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-port=7459/tcp, then sudo firewall-cmd --reload.
  • Synology: Control Panel › Security › Firewall.
  • QNAP: if QuFirewall is on, allow TCP port 7459 from your home network in the profile in use, under QuFirewall › Firewall Profiles. The QNAP steps say how.
  • Windows: set your home network to Private, and allow Docker Desktop Backend on private networks only. The note for Mac and Windows shows how, and what to do if you chose Cancel.
  • macOS: allow Docker when macOS asks whether it may accept incoming connections.

The firewall can also keep the helper away from Plex on the same machine. The status page then says "Plex Media Server: Not found". The helper asks Plex from its own Docker network, not from your home network. So a rule for your home network does not let it in, and PLEX_URL does not change that. Pair with the pairing code, which works just as well. Or let the helper's Docker network reach port 32400, and nothing else. This command shows the network's range:

docker network inspect --format '{{range .IPAM.Config}}{{.Subnet}} {{end}}' phlix-camera-upload_default

The network is named after the helper's folder. If Docker says that there is no such network, write your folder's name in place of phlix-camera-upload. Then allow that range. With ufw, put it in place of RANGE: sudo ufw allow from RANGE to any port 32400 proto tcp. On Synology, add a rule under Control Panel › Security › Firewall › Edit Rules: ports Custom, TCP 32400, source IP Specific IP with that range, action Allow. Drag it above any rule that denies. After docker compose down, Docker may give the helper another range, so check it again then.

Guest Wi-Fi, "client isolation", or a separate network for phones or smart devices (VLANs)

Guest networks and routers with "AP isolation" or "client isolation" keep devices from talking to each other. That is their job, and it stops the phone from reaching the server.

  • The simple fix: put the phone on the same network as the server.
  • If you keep phones and servers on separate networks on purpose, add one rule in your router or firewall: allow the phones' network to reach the server's address on TCP port 7459. Phlix needs nothing else, and no discovery traffic has to cross between the networks. Then type the server's address into Phlix.

Mesh Wi-Fi, several access points and a server on a cable are all fine, as long as they are one network.

Phlix says "Paused: not on your home network", but I am at home

In Phlix, open Settings › Camera Upload and tap This Is My Home Network. Phlix adds the network you are on, but only if your helper answers from it, with the certificate it had when you paired. A network where your helper does not answer is never added.

Why it happens: Phlix knows your home by the address range of the Wi-Fi network your phone was on when you paired, for example 192.168.1.0/24. It never reads the Wi-Fi's name and needs no location permission. If you changed your router, moved to another network at home, or have two networks, the range no longer matches.

A new router usually gives the server a new address as well. Phlix then says "The helper didn't answer". In that case choose Disconnect, then connect again, as in Connect your iPhone. Leave the folder name as Phlix shows it: the phone keeps its folder, and nothing is uploaded twice.

The iPhone's own settings: Local Network, VPNs
  • Local Network. iOS asks once whether Phlix may find devices on your local network. If the answer was no, Phlix cannot reach anything at home. Open the iPhone's Settings › Apps › Phlix (or Privacy & Security › Local Network) and switch on Local Network. Phlix tells you when this is the reason.
  • VPN apps. Many VPN apps block the local network while they are on. Look for a setting named "Allow LAN access" or "Local network sharing", or pause the VPN at home. iCloud Private Relay does not get in the way.
  • Mobile data. Phlix never uploads over mobile data. Low Data Mode on your home Wi-Fi does not hold uploads back: they stay on your own network.
My server's address changes

Give the server a fixed address: in your router, look for "DHCP reservation" or "static lease" and reserve the address the server has now. That helps Plex as much as the helper.

If the address does change, Phlix looks for the helper again at your Plex server's new address by itself, and carries on once it finds the same helper there. You can also disconnect and connect again; nothing is uploaded twice.

Away from home: remote access, VPNs into your home, port forwarding

Camera Upload is made for your home network, on purpose. Phlix checks the Wi-Fi network the phone is on, so a VPN into your home from somewhere else does not start uploads, and neither does Plex Remote Access.

One case looks different. Phlix knows your home by its address range, and a network somewhere else can use the same range: many routers use 192.168.1.x. Phlix takes such a network for home and tries your helper's address. No server there shows your helper's certificate, so no photo is sent. While the phone is on that network, the Camera Upload screen says "Can't reach the helper". The cause is that network, not your helper at home. Backing up carries on when the phone is back on your home Wi-Fi.

Do not forward port 7459 to the internet. It would add nothing, because Phlix only uploads at home, and it would expose a service on your server to everyone.

A reverse proxy, your own domain name, or your own certificate

You do not need any of these. If you already run them at home and prefer a name such as https://uploads.home.example:

  • Your own certificate: put the certificate and its key in the helper's config folder. Set TLS_CERT and TLS_KEY to their paths inside the container, where that folder is /config: for example /config/my-cert.pem and /config/my-key.pem. In Phlix, type the name with the helper's port: https://uploads.home.example:7459. With a certificate that iOS already trusts, Phlix uses iOS's own check, so a renewed certificate needs no new pairing.
  • A reverse proxy (Nginx Proxy Manager, Traefik, Caddy): set TLS: "off" on the helper and let the proxy add HTTPS. Phlix connects only over HTTPS. Lift the proxy's upload size limit and switch off request buffering, or large videos fail: in Nginx, client_max_body_size 0; and proxy_request_buffering off;.
  • With a reverse proxy, type its full address in Phlix: https://uploads.home.example.
  • Keep it inside your home. Do not route uploads through a cloud proxy: it would see your photos, and most limit uploads to 100 MB.

The home-network rule stays the same: Phlix uploads only on your home Wi-Fi, whatever the address.

Docker network modes: host, macvlan, Unraid's br0
  • Bridge with a published port is what the Compose file uses, and what works everywhere.
  • Host networking works too: replace the ports: and extra_hosts: lines with network_mode: host.
  • macvlan, ipvlan or Unraid's br0 give the helper its own address on your network. Type that address into Phlix. A container on such a network usually cannot reach the server it runs on, so set PLEX_URL to an address it can reach, or pair with the pairing code.
  • Docker inside WSL 2 without Docker Desktop: Windows does not pass connections from your network into WSL by default. Use Docker Desktop, or WSL's mirrored networking mode.

The helper uses IPv4. A home network with only IPv6 is not supported yet.

Still stuck? Write to support and paste what doctor printed. It holds no passwords and no file names.

Update, change or remove the helper

The helper has no settings screen. Everything is one file, docker-compose.yml, and one command to apply it: docker compose up -d, in the helper's folder.

The helper's folder is the folder that holds docker-compose.yml. If you used way A, it is phlix-camera-upload in your home folder: type cd ~/phlix-camera-upload to go there. If an AI assistant installed the helper (way B), its last message names the folder. If you used way C, it is the folder you made in its step 2.

On QNAP, the QNAP steps say how to make these changes in Container Station.

Update the helper 
docker compose pull
docker compose up -d

Run both in the helper's folder. Phones stay paired: the helper keeps its data in the config folder, and an update does not touch it. On Unraid, use Check for Updates on the Docker tab. In Phlix, the Helper Version row on the Camera Upload screen shows which version is running, and the screen says Your helper needs an update for a helper older than 1.0.2. What changed in each version is on the releases page on GitHub.

Move the upload folder

Stop the helper with docker compose down, move the folder on the server, change the source: line in docker-compose.yml, and run docker compose up -d. Phones stay paired, and nothing is uploaded twice. Remember that the new place must be inside a Plex photo library too.

The name of a device's folder

Phlix shows the folder in use on the Camera Upload screen, next to Folder. To rename it, rename the folder on the server: the helper finds it there and puts new photos in it. Or choose Disconnect, connect again and type a new name: new photos go into a folder of that name, and photos already on the server stay where they are.

docker compose down

Removing the helper never deletes photos. Everything it received stays in your upload folder. To remove the helper's own data as well, delete the folder you made for it. In Phlix, switch Camera Upload off or choose Disconnect.

Questions

Is Camera Upload a beta?

Yes. Camera Upload is a beta in Phlix 1.0.21 or later. It does what the list on this page says. Keep your own backup of anything you cannot replace, and tell us what goes wrong: in Phlix, open Settings and tap Send Feedback, or use the support page.

What happened to Plex's own Camera Upload?

Plex announced in March 2021 that it was ending support for its Camera Upload, with removal targeted for June 2021. Camera Upload in Phlix is a separate feature of an independent app: it copies your photos into a folder of your Plex photo library through the helper on your own server. The whole story, with Plex's announcement.

Does it delete photos from my iPhone?

No. Phlix copies your photos and videos to your server. It never deletes or changes anything in your Photo Library. Freeing space on the phone stays your decision.

Does it work away from home, or over mobile data?

No, on purpose. Phlix uploads only while the phone is on your home Wi-Fi. Away from home it does not start uploads or look for your server, apart from rare cases that the privacy policy lists; even then, your photos go only to your own helper. Photos you take on a trip upload when you are back.

Do I need Plex Pass?

No. The helper writes ordinary files into a folder of your Plex photo library, and that works with every Plex Media Server. The photos then show in your Plex photo library, and in Phlix.

What does it cost?

The helper for your server is free. In the app, the first 100 uploads are free, so you can see it work from end to end. Phlix Pro uploads the rest, and what you take from then on.

Can the whole family use it?

Yes. Each iPhone and iPad pairs by itself and gets its own folder. The owner of the Plex server pairs without a code; everyone else types the helper's pairing code. If two phones hold the same photo, the server stores it once.

How long does the first backup take?

With Phlix open on the Camera Upload screen and the phone on its charger, it runs as fast as your Wi-Fi allows. With Phlix closed, iOS gives it short bursts, mostly while the phone charges, so a large library takes a few days. Either way you can see the count rise in the app.

Is this a sync? What if I delete a photo?

It is a one-way copy, from the phone to the server. Deleting a photo on the phone leaves the copy on the server, and nothing comes back to the phone. If you delete a file on the server and the photo is still on the phone, Phlix uploads it again.

Does it work with iCloud Photos?

Yes. Phlix reads the photos in your Photo Library, wherever iOS keeps them. With "Optimize iPhone Storage", iOS downloads the full-size original from iCloud when Phlix asks for it, and Phlix needs about 300 MB free on the phone to get each file ready. Shared albums are not included.

Is there an Android version?

No. Phlix is an app for iPhone and iPad.

Privacy and security

  • Your photos stay on your network. The phone sends them straight to your server. Phlix, the developer, never receives them and cannot reach them. The helper contacts nothing on the internet; the only thing it calls is your own Plex Media Server.
  • The connection is encrypted. When a phone pairs, Phlix remembers the helper's exact certificate. From then on it talks only to a server that shows it.
  • Pairing needs proof. The owner of the Plex server proves it through Plex, and the helper never stores a Plex token. Everyone else needs the pairing code, and the helper slows down anyone who guesses.
  • A paired phone can only add. The helper offers no way to list, read, download, overwrite or delete a stored file. It accepts photo and video files only.
  • You can read the code. The helper's source is public on GitHub, with the security notes.

The privacy policy has the full description.
