Stream Plex Photos to Your TV with AirPlay

Your photos deserve a bigger screen. Here is how to get your Plex photos onto your TV using AirPlay -- and how to make them look their best.

September 22, 2026

You have thousands of photos on your Plex server. Your TV is right there, with a beautiful 55-inch or 65-inch screen that could be displaying those photos instead of sitting idle. The connection between those two things should be simple, and with the right setup it is.

This guide covers every method for displaying Plex AirPlay photos on your TV, from basic screen mirroring to a dedicated app that puts just the photo on the TV. Whether you want to show a few vacation photos to your family or run a continuous photo display in your living room, you will find the right approach here.

Why AirPlay for Plex Photos?

Before we get into the how, let us address the why. Browsing photos with a TV remote -- clicking through albums, scrolling grids, selecting individual photos -- takes a lot of clicks, and a phone touchscreen is a more natural way to choose what to display.

AirPlay flips the model. You browse and control from your iPhone -- where touch-based photo navigation feels natural -- and the TV displays what you choose. Your phone is the controller. Your TV is the display. Each device does what it does best.

Method 1: Screen Mirroring from the Plex App

The simplest way to get Plex photos on your TV using AirPlay is screen mirroring. Here is the step-by-step process:

  1. Make sure your iPhone and Apple TV (or AirPlay 2-compatible TV) are on the same Wi-Fi network.
  2. Open Control Center on your iPhone (swipe down from the top-right corner).
  3. Tap "Screen Mirroring" and select your TV from the list.
  4. Open the Plex app and navigate to your photo library.
  5. Browse or start a slideshow. Everything on your phone screen appears on the TV.

What You Get

Screen mirroring works immediately with no additional apps or setup. It displays whatever is on your iPhone screen, so any photo you view in the Plex app appears on the TV.

What You Lose

Screen mirroring has real drawbacks for photos specifically:

  • The TV shows your phone's interface, not just the photo. Status bar, navigation chrome, toolbar -- all of it appears on the TV. During a slideshow, your audience sees whatever the app shows on your phone, controls included.
  • Resolution is capped at your phone's screen resolution. Your iPhone's display is roughly 1170x2532 pixels. When this is mirrored to a large TV, the image is scaled up and loses sharpness. A 24-megapixel photo is squeezed into your phone's screen resolution first.
  • Your phone is locked. You cannot use your phone for anything else while mirroring. A text message notification will appear on the TV. Switching apps interrupts the display.
  • Battery drain. Screen mirroring continuously encodes and transmits video from your phone. Expect significant battery consumption during extended viewing.

Screen mirroring works for a quick "look at this photo" moment. For anything longer, such as a slideshow during a dinner party or a photo display running for an evening, a better method exists.

Method 2: Phlix during Screen Mirroring -- the TV shows only the photo

Mirroring does not have to mean a copy of your phone screen. While Screen Mirroring is on, iOS hands an app the TV as a second screen, and an app built for it can draw its own full-screen window there while the phone shows something else, like playback controls. This is how presentation apps work, and it is the ideal approach for displaying Plex photos on a TV.

To use this method, you need an app built for it, such as Phlix, an independent app that works with Plex Media Server.

Setting Up AirPlay Photos Step by Step

What You Need

  • An AirPlay 2 receiver: an Apple TV, or an AirPlay 2-compatible smart TV from Samsung (2018+), LG (2019+), Sony (2019+), or Vizio (2018+).
  • An iPhone running iOS 17 or later.
  • A Plex Media Server with a photo library configured. If you have not set this up yet, our Plex photo library setup guide walks through the process.
  • Both devices on the same Wi-Fi network. For best results, connect the Apple TV via Ethernet and keep the iPhone on Wi-Fi.

Setup Steps with Phlix

  1. Download Phlix from the App Store and sign in to your Plex account.
  2. Browse your photo timeline and find the photos you want to display.
  3. Open Control Center, tap Screen Mirroring and pick your TV (or plug in an HDMI adapter). Phlix notices the connected display by itself.
  4. Start a slideshow. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen.
  5. Use your iPhone to control playback: pause, skip forward or back. Closing the player ends the show on the TV.

Getting the Best Quality

The visual quality of your AirPlay photos depends on several factors you can control:

Source Photo Resolution

Phlix asks your Plex server for an image sized to the display. The more detail your original has, the better that image can be: a 24-megapixel photo from a modern camera (6000x4000) or a 12-megapixel phone photo (4032x3024) has plenty for a big TV. Even older photos at lower resolutions look better on a TV than you might expect because the typical viewing distance (8 to 12 feet) compensates for lower pixel density.

Network Speed

Each photo travels from your Plex server to your iPhone before it reaches the TV. On a fast home network that is quick; on slower networks, there may be a brief delay between photos while the next image loads. Connecting your Plex server and Apple TV via Ethernet (rather than Wi-Fi) ensures the most reliable performance.

Pre-Generate Thumbnails

While Phlix asks for display-sized images for the TV, it uses Plex thumbnails for the grid and timeline on your iPhone. Make sure your Plex server has fully generated thumbnails for your photo library. See our guide on optimizing Plex for large photo libraries for details on pre-generating thumbnails.

Practical Scenarios

Family Gathering

In the week before the event, copy the photos you want to show into one folder on your server, with a subfolder per year if you like. When family arrives, start a slideshow of that folder on the TV; with Include Subfolders on, it plays the folders inside it too. Set the interval to 6 seconds and let it run all evening. For more on this use case, see our family photo slideshow guide.

Background Photo Display

Set the longest interval (10 seconds per photo) and shuffle your entire library. Your TV becomes a slowly rotating photo gallery -- like a digital photo frame, but with access to your full Plex library of thousands of photos instead of the 50 that fit on a frame's internal memory.

Photo Review Session

After a vacation or event, start a slideshow of the folder with your new photos and pause on any photo to look at it longer. Phlix asks your server for an image sized to the TV, so you can evaluate sharpness, composition, and color on a proper screen.

Troubleshooting AirPlay Photo Issues

TV not appearing as an AirPlay target. Verify both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network. Restart the Apple TV or smart TV. On Apple TV, go to Settings, then AirPlay and HomeKit, and confirm AirPlay is enabled. On smart TVs, check that AirPlay is turned on in the TV's settings menu. For more AirPlay troubleshooting, see our comprehensive Plex AirPlay guide.

Photos loading slowly between slides. This usually indicates a network bottleneck between your Plex server and the iPhone. Connect the Plex server via Ethernet. If the server is on a NAS, verify it is on a gigabit (or faster) connection to your router.

Photos appear cropped on TV. Photos are scaled to fit the screen. A portrait-orientation photo on a landscape TV will have black bars on the sides -- this is correct and preserves the photo's proportions. Photos are never cropped or distorted.

iPhone battery draining quickly. Screen Mirroring does consume battery. For extended viewing sessions, keep your iPhone plugged in. A Lightning or USB-C cable to a charger ensures the slideshow can run indefinitely.
