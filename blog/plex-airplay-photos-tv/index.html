Stream Plex Photos to Your TV with AirPlay

Your photos deserve a bigger screen. Here is how to get your Plex photos onto your TV using AirPlay -- and how to make them look their best.

September 22, 2026

You have thousands of photos on your Plex server. Your TV is right there, with a beautiful 55-inch or 65-inch screen that could be displaying those photos instead of sitting idle. The connection between those two things should be simple, but Plex does not make it easy. The official Plex app was not designed to send photos to your TV via AirPlay in any optimized way, and the result is that most Plex users never see their photos on their biggest screen.

This guide covers every method for displaying Plex AirPlay photos on your TV, from basic screen mirroring to dedicated apps that use AirPlay 2 extended display for full-resolution output. Whether you want to show a few vacation photos to your family or run a continuous photo display in your living room, you will find the right approach here.

Why AirPlay for Plex Photos?

Before we get into the how, let us address the why. The Plex app exists natively on Apple TV, so you can browse photos directly on the TV without AirPlay. However, the Plex app on Apple TV has significant limitations for photos:

  • No chronological timeline. You navigate album by album, just like on mobile. There is no unified photo timeline on the Apple TV Plex app.
  • Sluggish navigation with a remote. Browsing photos with the Apple TV remote -- clicking through albums, scrolling grids, selecting individual photos -- is tedious. A phone touchscreen is a far more natural way to choose what to display.
  • Limited slideshow. The Apple TV Plex app's slideshow is album-bound with minimal controls.

AirPlay flips the model. You browse and control from your iPhone -- where touch-based photo navigation feels natural -- and the TV displays what you choose. Your phone is the controller. Your TV is the display. Each device does what it does best.

Method 1: Screen Mirroring from the Plex App

The simplest way to get Plex photos on your TV using AirPlay is screen mirroring. Here is the step-by-step process:

  1. Make sure your iPhone and Apple TV (or AirPlay 2-compatible TV) are on the same Wi-Fi network.
  2. Open Control Center on your iPhone (swipe down from the top-right corner).
  3. Tap "Screen Mirroring" and select your TV from the list.
  4. Open the Plex app and navigate to your photo library.
  5. Browse or start a slideshow. Everything on your phone screen appears on the TV.

What You Get

Screen mirroring works immediately with no additional apps or setup. It displays whatever is on your iPhone screen, so any photo you view in the Plex app appears on the TV.

What You Lose

Screen mirroring has real drawbacks for photos specifically:

  • The TV shows your phone's interface, not just the photo. Status bar, navigation chrome, toolbar -- all of it appears on the TV. During a slideshow, your audience sees the Plex app interface rather than a clean, full-screen photo.
  • Resolution is capped at your phone's screen resolution. Your iPhone's display is roughly 1170x2532 pixels. When this is mirrored to a 4K TV (3840x2160), the image is upscaled and loses sharpness. A 24-megapixel photo that could display beautifully at 4K gets compressed through your phone's screen resolution first.
  • Your phone is locked. You cannot use your phone for anything else while mirroring. A text message notification will appear on the TV. Switching apps interrupts the display.
  • Battery drain. Screen mirroring continuously encodes and transmits video from your phone. Expect significant battery consumption during extended viewing.

Screen mirroring works for a quick "look at this photo" moment. For anything longer, such as a slideshow during a dinner party or a photo display running for an evening, a better method exists.

Method 2: AirPlay 2 Extended Display

Extended display is fundamentally different from mirroring. Instead of duplicating your phone's screen, extended display treats the TV as an independent second screen. The TV shows one thing (your photo, full-screen, edge to edge) while your phone shows something else (controls, thumbnails, the next photo in the queue). This is how professional presentation apps work, and it is the ideal approach for displaying Plex photos on a TV.

The native Plex app does not support extended display for photos. To use this method, you need an app specifically built for it.

Phlix: Full-Resolution AirPlay Photos on Your TV

Phlix uses AirPlay 2 extended display to deliver a true dual-screen experience for Plex photos. Here is what happens when you connect:

On the TV: Your photo fills the entire screen at the TV's native resolution -- up to 4K on Apple TV 4K. No status bar, no navigation chrome, no app interface. Just the photo, edge to edge, exactly as a dedicated digital photo frame would display it. Hard cuts transition cleanly between photos.

On your iPhone: Phlix shows the current photo, a strip of upcoming photos, and playback controls. You can pause, skip, adjust the display duration, shuffle, and browse your timeline -- all without affecting what is showing on the TV. Your phone is a dedicated remote for the photo display.

Pick up your phone to skip a photo or jump to a different year, then set it down again. Your audience never sees anything but photos on the big screen.

Setting Up AirPlay Photos Step by Step

What You Need

  • An AirPlay 2 receiver: Apple TV 4K (any generation) for the best experience. Apple TV HD works at 1080p. AirPlay 2-compatible smart TVs from Samsung (2018+), LG (2019+), Sony (2019+), and Vizio (2018+) also work.
  • An iPhone running iOS 17 or later.
  • A Plex Media Server with a photo library configured. If you have not set this up yet, our Plex photo library setup guide walks through the process.
  • Both devices on the same Wi-Fi network. For best results, connect the Apple TV via Ethernet and keep the iPhone on Wi-Fi.

Setup Steps with Phlix

  1. Download Phlix from the App Store and sign in to your Plex account.
  2. Browse your photo timeline and find the photos you want to display.
  3. Connect to your AirPlay 2 display. Phlix detects available AirPlay receivers automatically.
  4. Start a slideshow. The TV immediately begins showing photos at full resolution.
  5. Use your iPhone to control playback: adjust timing, skip photos, shuffle, or browse to a different part of your library.

Getting the Best Quality

The visual quality of your AirPlay photos depends on several factors you can control:

Source Photo Resolution

Phlix fetches the original full-resolution file from your Plex server for TV display. A 24-megapixel photo from a modern camera (6000x4000) will look stunning at 4K. Phone photos at 12 megapixels (4032x3024) display beautifully. Even older photos at lower resolutions look better on a TV than you might expect because the typical viewing distance (8 to 12 feet) compensates for lower pixel density.

Network Speed

Full-resolution photos can be 5 to 15 MB each. If your Plex server is connected via Ethernet and your network is reasonably modern, each photo loads in under a second. On slower networks, there may be a brief delay between photos while the next image loads. Connecting your Plex server and Apple TV via Ethernet (rather than Wi-Fi) ensures the most reliable performance.

Pre-Generate Thumbnails

While Phlix fetches full-resolution images for TV display, it uses Plex thumbnails for the preview strip on your iPhone. Make sure your Plex server has fully generated thumbnails for your photo library. See our guide on optimizing Plex for large photo libraries for details on pre-generating thumbnails.

Practical Scenarios

Family Gathering

Mark your favorite photos across different years as favorites in Phlix throughout the week before the event. When family arrives, start a favorites-only slideshow on the TV. Set the interval to 8 seconds and let it run all evening. For more on this use case, see our family photo slideshow guide.

Background Photo Display

Set a long interval (15 to 30 seconds per photo) and shuffle your entire library. Your TV becomes a slowly rotating photo gallery -- like a digital photo frame, but with access to your full Plex library of thousands of photos instead of the 50 that fit on a frame's internal memory.

Photo Review Session

After a vacation or event, use Phlix to display your recent photos on the TV while reviewing them. On your phone, swipe through the timeline and mark favorites. On the TV, each photo appears at full 4K resolution so you can evaluate sharpness, composition, and color on a proper screen.

Troubleshooting AirPlay Photo Issues

TV not appearing as an AirPlay target. Verify both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network. Restart the Apple TV or smart TV. On Apple TV, go to Settings, then AirPlay and HomeKit, and confirm AirPlay is enabled. On smart TVs, check that AirPlay is turned on in the TV's settings menu. For more AirPlay troubleshooting, see our comprehensive Plex AirPlay guide.

Photos loading slowly between slides. This usually indicates a network bottleneck between your Plex server and the iPhone. Connect the Plex server via Ethernet. If the server is on a NAS, verify it is on a gigabit (or faster) connection to your router.

Photos appear cropped on TV. Photos are displayed at full resolution with aspect-ratio scaling. A portrait-orientation photo on a landscape TV will have black bars on the sides -- this is correct and preserves the photo's proportions. Photos are never cropped or distorted.

iPhone battery draining quickly. AirPlay extended display does consume battery. For extended viewing sessions, keep your iPhone plugged in. A Lightning or USB-C cable to a charger ensures the slideshow can run indefinitely.
