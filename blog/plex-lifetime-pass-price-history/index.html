Plex Lifetime Pass Price History — From $75 to $749

By Jason Loomis · September 1, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026 · 8 min read

The price of the Plex Pass Lifetime subscription has gone up several times. In January 2014 it was $74.99; since July 1, 2026 it has been $749.99, ten times as much. Here is the price timeline, with Plex's own pages as the sources, what Plex said about the increases, community reaction, and an honest assessment of whether the current price is still worth it.

Why Does Plex Keep Raising the Price?

When it announced the April 2025 increase, Plex said it needed to keep up with rising costs and keep investing in new features. When it announced the July 2026 increase, Plex said the new price reflects the value of the software, and that it had considered retiring the Lifetime pass, because recurring subscriptions help it sustain long-term development, but chose to keep it. Two more reasons are not hard to infer:

Lifetime passes are a financial liability

Every lifetime subscriber is someone who pays once but uses server infrastructure, support resources, and content delivery indefinitely. As Plex adds cloud-dependent features (Discover, Watchlist, ad-supported streaming, relay servers), the ongoing cost of serving each user increases. A user who paid $74.99 in 2014 and still uses Plex daily in 2026 has been using twelve years of infrastructure for the cost of a nice dinner.

The annual plan costs less for most people

At $749, the annual plan at $69.99/year costs less for anyone who expects to use Plex for less than about ten and a half years.

Existing lifetime subscribers are grandfathered

Price increases do not affect existing lifetime pass holders. If you bought at $75 or $119, you still have full Plex Pass access. This creates a two-tier community: early adopters who got an incredible deal and newcomers facing a very different value proposition.

Community Reaction

Every price increase has triggered significant pushback on r/PleX and the Plex forums. The most common reactions:

  • "I bought at $75 and it is the best software purchase I have ever made." Early adopters are overwhelmingly positive. After 10+ years of use, the per-year cost is under $7.
  • "At $749, I would rather switch to Jellyfin." Newcomers increasingly view Jellyfin (which is completely free) as the alternative rather than paying $749. This sentiment has accelerated Jellyfin's growth.
  • "The annual plan is fine." Many pragmatic users point out that $69.99/year is reasonable for the features provided, and you can cancel anytime. The lifetime pass is only worth discussing if you plan to use Plex for 10+ years.
  • "Plex is pushing people away." Some long-time community members view the pricing as a sign that Plex is prioritizing revenue over the self-hosting community that built its user base.

Value Analysis at Each Price Point

At $74.99 (2014): Incredible value

Anyone who bought at this price got one of the best deals in software history. Twelve years of full Plex Pass features for $74.99 works out to about $6.25 per year. Hardware transcoding alone saves more than that in electricity costs on a busy server.

At $119.99 (until April 2025): Strong value

At this price, the break-even point versus the annual plan, then $39.99 a year, was about three years. Anyone who expected to use Plex for longer came out ahead with lifetime. This was the sweet spot where most r/PleX users recommended buying lifetime without hesitation.

At $249.99 (April 2025 to June 2026): Reasonable for committed users

Break-even against the annual plan, now $69.99 a year: about 3.5 years. Still a good deal for users who knew they would stick with Plex, but no longer an impulse buy. The increased price made the annual plan more attractive for users who were not sure about their long-term commitment.

At $749.99 (from July 2026): Only for diehards

Break-even against the annual plan: approximately 10.7 years. You need to use Plex continuously until 2037 to come out ahead versus paying annually. That is a very long time in technology. Could Plex pivot, shut down, or be replaced by something better in a decade? Absolutely.

At $749, the lifetime pass is no longer a smart financial bet for most users. It is a statement of commitment to the platform, or a bet that Plex will continue to exist and matter for over a decade. For most people, the annual plan at $69.99/year is the rational choice. If you want a longer commitment than a year without the lifetime gamble, the $249.99 five-year pass launched with the 2026 increase fills that gap.

Alternatives to Consider

The rising cost of Plex Pass has driven many users to explore alternatives for specific features:

  • Hardware transcoding: Jellyfin offers this for free. If hardware transcoding is your primary reason for Plex Pass, switching servers eliminates the cost entirely.
  • Photo management: Phlix provides photo browsing, timeline navigation, slideshows on your TV, and saves to the Photos app for your Plex library; no Plex Pass is needed on your home network. Phlix Pro costs $6.99/year or $19.99 lifetime -- a fraction of Plex Pass.
  • Music: Plexamp is excellent but requires Plex Pass. Navidrome is a free, self-hosted alternative that works with Subsonic-compatible music apps.
  • DVR: Jellyfin and TVHeadend offer free DVR functionality with OTA tuners.

Will the Price Keep Rising?

Plex raised it in April 2025 and again in July 2026. There is no way to know from the outside whether it will rise again.

If you are on the fence about Plex Pass, the annual plan is the safest bet. You get full features, you can cancel anytime, and you are not exposed to the risk of overpaying for a decade-long commitment you might not keep.

The Bottom Line

The Plex Lifetime Pass was one of the best deals in self-hosted software -- past tense. At $75 or $119, it was an easy recommendation. At $749, it is a niche purchase for users with deep commitment to the platform. For everyone else, the $69.99/year annual plan provides the same features with far less financial risk. And for specific use cases like photo browsing, independent apps like Phlix, which works with Plex Media Server, offer the features you need at a fraction of the cost.
