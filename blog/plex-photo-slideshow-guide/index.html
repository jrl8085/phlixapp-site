Create Beautiful Slideshows from Your Plex Photo Library

Your Plex server has years of photos. Here is how to turn them into slideshows worth watching -- on your phone, on your TV, or both at once.

September 22, 2026

A photo sitting in a folder on your Plex server is a photo nobody sees. Slideshows change that. They turn a static archive into something alive -- a stream of memories playing on your TV during a family dinner, or scrolling on your phone while you wait for coffee.

This guide covers creating a Plex photo slideshow: starting one in Plex Web, what makes a great slideshow, and how to build slideshows that span your entire photo collection, play on your TV, and actually look beautiful.

Plex's Built-In Photo Slideshow

Plex Web has a slideshow for photo libraries (Plex: Using the Library View). Here is how to start one.

How to Start a Plex Slideshow

  1. Open your photo library in Plex Web (app.plex.tv).
  2. Choose a folder or album, then open a photo.
  3. Use the Play button to start the slideshow.
  4. The slideshow begins, cycling through photos in the album automatically.

For a whole-library slideshow on your living room TV, a highlight reel for a family gathering, or a photo display that runs all evening, here is what to look for.

What Makes a Great Photo Slideshow

Before diving into solutions, it is worth defining what a great Plex photo slideshow actually looks like:

Cross-Album Playback

A slideshow should be able to pull photos from your entire library, not just one folder. Your memories do not live in silos, and your slideshow should not either. The best slideshows mix vacation shots from 2019 with birthday photos from 2023 and candid moments from last week.

Sharp on the Big Screen

If you are showing photos on a big TV, the slideshow should show them at the TV's resolution. Upscaled phone-resolution images look soft and washed out on a 55-inch screen. A good slideshow asks your Plex server for an image as large as the display can show.

Clean Cuts

Each photo should fill the screen cleanly, with a simple hard cut to the next image. The focus should be on the photos themselves, not on distracting visual effects between them.

Controllable Pacing

Different situations call for different timing. A quick review of recent photos might use 3-second intervals. A background display during a dinner party works better at 8 to 10 seconds per photo. The slideshow should let you adjust this easily.

Curated Selection

Not every photo deserves slideshow time. Being able to play just a chosen set -- one folder of your best shots, or a single year -- transforms the experience from "every photo I have ever taken" to "the highlights of my life."

Building a Plex Photo Slideshow with Phlix

Phlix is an independent app that works with Plex Media Server. Here is how its slideshows work.

Whole-Library Slideshows

Start a slideshow from the timeline and it plays your whole library in date order, crossing album boundaries automatically, regardless of which folders the photos live in on your server. Pick a year with the year scrubber first, and the slideshow plays just that year. Start one in a folder, and it plays that folder and, with Include Subfolders on, the folders inside it.

A Folder of Your Best Shots

Phlix does not edit or move what is on your server, so curate on the server itself: copy your best shots into one folder (a subfolder per event works too) and start the slideshow there. That gives guests a highlight reel without every throwaway photo in your library.

On-Device and External Display

Phlix supports two slideshow modes. The on-device slideshow plays full-screen on your iPhone -- great for personal viewing. The other is for the TV. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen. Adjustable Timing

Choose 2, 4, 6 or 10 seconds per photo before the show starts. Running a slideshow as ambient background art? Pick 10. Reviewing photos quickly? Pick 2.

Shuffle

Enable shuffle to randomize the order across your entire collection. This creates a "random memories" experience similar to Apple Photos' "For You" feature, but sourced entirely from your self-hosted Plex library.

Tips for Better Plex Photo Slideshows

Regardless of which app you use, these tips will improve your slideshow quality:

Organize Photos by Date

Slideshows are most engaging when they flow chronologically. Make sure your photos have accurate EXIF date metadata so they sort correctly. If you have scanned old prints or downloaded images without proper dates, tools like ExifTool can help you set dates manually. For more on organizing your photo library, see our Plex photo library setup guide.

Pre-Generate Thumbnails

If your Plex server has not generated thumbnails for all your photos, the slideshow may pause or stutter as it waits for the server to create thumbnails on demand. Run a full library scan and let Plex complete all thumbnail generation before your first slideshow. Our guide on browsing large Plex photo libraries covers this process in detail.

Use a Wired Server Connection

Photos can be 5 to 15 MB each. If your Plex server is on Wi-Fi, loading each photo for the slideshow may take noticeably longer than on a wired Ethernet connection. A wired server helps each photo arrive in time for its slide.

Curate Before You Present

Spend 10 minutes before a family event copying the best shots into one folder on your server. When it is time to show photos, start a slideshow of that folder instead of playing through everything. The difference in quality is dramatic -- your audience sees only your best memories, not the five nearly identical photos you took trying to get everyone's eyes open.

Plex Photo Slideshow on Your TV

The most impactful way to experience a photo slideshow is on a large screen. A 55-inch or 65-inch TV turns your photos into an experience that a phone screen simply cannot match. There are two approaches to getting your Plex photo slideshow onto your TV:

  • Screen mirroring. Mirror your phone's screen to the TV via AirPlay. Quick and easy, but the TV shows your phone interface (navigation bars, status bar, notifications) along with the photos. Resolution is limited to your phone's screen resolution, not the TV's native resolution.
  • Phlix during Screen Mirroring (or over HDMI). With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen. Phlix asks your server for an image sized to the display.

For a detailed comparison of these approaches and setup instructions, see our guide to streaming Plex photos to your TV with AirPlay.

The Bottom Line

Your Plex photo library is a treasure, and a good slideshow is how you actually enjoy it: a folder, a year, or the whole library, at the pace you choose, on the biggest screen in the house.
