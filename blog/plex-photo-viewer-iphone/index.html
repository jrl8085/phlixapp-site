How to Browse and View Your Plex Photos on iPhone

Your Plex server holds thousands of photos. Here is how to actually browse them on your iPhone -- and why the right photo viewer makes all the difference.

September 22, 2026

You have spent hours setting up your Plex Media Server, organizing folders, and adding your photo collection. Now you want to pull out your iPhone and browse those Plex photos the same way you scroll through your Camera Roll. Simple, right? In theory, yes. In practice, the experience depends entirely on which app you use to view them.

Plex Inc. makes its own app for photo libraries, and independent apps that work with Plex Media Server are another option; the one that suits you depends on your library and how you like to browse.

This guide walks through the options for browsing and viewing Plex photos on your iPhone, from Plex's own apps to independent photo apps, so you can choose the approach that fits your library size and browsing habits.

Viewing Plex Photos in Plex's Own App

Plex Inc.'s app for photo libraries on iPhone and iPad is Plex Photos. Its App Store page lists three supported features: Recommended View, Timeline View and Library View. Its latest version is 2025.2.0, from June 12, 2025.

Using the Plex Web App on iPhone

You can also open your photo library in Plex Web at app.plex.tv in Safari, which is handy on a device where you have not installed an app.

What to Look For in a Photo App

If you use Plex specifically for photos -- whether it is your family photo archive, a photography portfolio, or decades of memories backed up to a NAS -- here is what to look for in a photo app:

Chronological Timeline

The most natural way to browse photos is by date. A proper photo viewer should display your entire Plex photo library as a single chronological timeline, regardless of how photos are organized into folders on your server. You should be able to scroll from today back to 2005 (or whenever your digital photos begin) in one continuous feed, just like Apple Photos or Google Photos.

Fast Thumbnail Loading

A photo browser that makes you wait for thumbnails defeats the purpose. The viewer should pre-fetch thumbnails, cache them aggressively, and use virtual scrolling so that performance stays consistent whether your library has 500 photos or 100,000.

Year Scrubber

When your library spans decades, scrolling from top to bottom is impractical. A year scrubber -- a small control on the side of the screen that lets you jump directly to any year -- turns a five-minute scroll into a one-second tap.

Favorites and Search

Mark photos you love, then access them in a single tap. Search by year, month, or album name to find specific memories quickly.

How Phlix Handles Large Libraries

Phlix uses server-side year filtering to keep API responses fast. Instead of loading metadata for 100,000 photos at once, it queries by year. When you scroll to 2021, only 2021's photos are fetched. Combined with virtual paging (only rendering photos visible on screen), this means a library of 100,000 photos is requested a year at a time, so each screen stays small. For a deeper look at these performance techniques, see our guide on browsing large Plex photo libraries without lag.

The Year Scrubber

Setting Up Your Photo Library in Plex

Regardless of which app you choose, the browsing experience starts with how your photos are organized on the server. If you have not set up your Plex photo library yet, or if your current setup is not performing well, our Plex photo library setup guide covers folder structure, EXIF metadata, and scan settings in detail.

The key principles: organize photos into year and month folders, ensure your EXIF dates are accurate (Plex uses these for chronological sorting), and store your Plex database on an SSD for fast thumbnail loading.

Which Option Is Right for You?

  • Casual viewer with a small library (under 1,000 photos): Plex's own app is a natural place to start, and browsing by album is manageable at this scale.
  • Moderate library (1,000 to 10,000 photos): A timeline and a year scrubber start to matter. Try a timeline-based app on your own library, such as Plex Photos (Timeline View) or Phlix.
  • Large library (10,000+ photos): Navigating hundreds of albums one by one becomes impractical, so pick an app with a timeline and a quick way to jump between years. Phlix is built for big libraries: it loads a page at a time instead of everything at once.

The Bottom Line

Plex photos on iPhone are only as good as the app you use to view them. If your photo library is a meaningful part of your Plex setup, pick an app built for photos.
