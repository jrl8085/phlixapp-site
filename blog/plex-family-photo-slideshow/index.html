How to Create a Family Photo Slideshow with Plex

Published September 1, 2026 · 9 min read

There is something special about seeing your family photos on a big screen. Holiday gatherings, birthday parties, vacations, everyday moments -- they all feel more vivid when displayed at full size rather than squinting at them on a phone. If your photos live on a Plex server, you already have the infrastructure for a family slideshow. The question is how to get the best experience, because Plex's native slideshow capabilities have real limitations.

This guide covers how to set up a photo library in Plex, the slideshow options available natively, the frustrations you are likely to encounter, and how Phlix solves those problems with a purpose-built photo browsing and slideshow experience.

Setting Up Your Photo Library in Plex

Before you can run a slideshow, your photos need to be in Plex. If you have not set this up yet, here is the quick version:

  1. Organize your photos on disk. Plex works best with photos organized into folders by date or event. A structure like Photos/2025/2025-12 Christmas/ keeps things clean and browsable. Plex reads EXIF data from your image files, so dates, camera info, and GPS coordinates are pulled in automatically.
  2. Create a Photo library in Plex. In the Plex Web app, go to Settings, then Manage Libraries, then Add Library. Choose "Photos" as the library type and point it at your photo folder.
  3. Let Plex scan. Plex will index your photos and generate thumbnails. For large libraries (tens of thousands of images), this initial scan can take several hours. Let it complete before trying to browse or create slideshows.

Supported Formats

Plex supports JPEG, PNG, TIFF, and several RAW formats (CR2, NEF, ARW, DNG, and others). JPEG is the most efficient for slideshows. RAW files work but generate larger thumbnails and take longer to process. HEIF/HEIC files from iPhones are also supported on most platforms.

Native Plex Slideshow Options

Plex has built-in slideshow functionality, but the experience varies dramatically depending on which client you use.

Plex Web App

In the web browser, navigate to a photo album and click the "Slideshow" button. Photos advance automatically at a fixed interval. You can pause, skip forward, and skip back. The web app slideshow is functional but basic. Photo resolution is limited by your browser window size, and there are no shuffle or timing controls.

Plex for Apple TV

The Apple TV app has a slideshow mode accessible from photo albums. It displays photos at the TV's native resolution, which is an improvement over the web app. However, the controls are limited. You cannot shuffle photos, adjust the display duration per slide, or easily navigate large libraries. The Apple TV Plex app is primarily built for video, and the photo experience feels like an afterthought.

Plex for iOS (iPhone and iPad)

The official Plex iOS app can display photos and has a basic slideshow function. For getting photos onto a TV, you would need to AirPlay or screen mirror from your phone, which introduces quality loss and ties up your phone for the duration. The app does not have dedicated slideshow controls for external display output.

The Limitations of Native Plex Slideshows

If you have tried to use Plex's built-in slideshow for a family gathering, you have probably encountered these frustrations:

  • No shuffle. Most Plex clients do not offer a shuffle option for slideshows. Photos play in the order they appear in the album, which is usually chronological. If you want a random mix of memories, you are out of luck.
  • No timing controls. You cannot adjust how long each photo displays. Some clients show each image for a fixed five or eight seconds, and that is not always the right pace. A detailed landscape might deserve fifteen seconds; a blurry action shot deserves three.
  • Limited library browsing. Plex's photo browsing interface on TV clients is clunky for large libraries. Scrolling through thousands of thumbnails with a TV remote is painfully slow. There is no year scrubber, no timeline view, and no fast way to jump to photos from a specific date or event.
  • No on-This-Day or smart selections. Plex does not surface photos from past years on the current date, which is one of the most beloved features in Apple Photos and Google Photos.
  • Screen mirroring quality loss. If you resort to screen mirroring your phone to show photos, the image quality is significantly reduced. A 24-megapixel photo gets compressed to your phone's screen resolution and then further compressed for wireless transmission. The result is noticeably softer than the original.

Tips for the Best Family Slideshow

  1. Curate an album first. Rather than playing your entire library, create a folder on your Plex server with your best photos from a trip, year, or event. A curated set of 50 to 100 photos makes a much better slideshow than 10,000 unsorted images.
  2. Use landscape photos for TV. Television screens are landscape (16:9). Portrait photos displayed on a TV have large black bars on the sides. Landscapes fill the screen edge to edge. If possible, favor landscape-oriented photos for TV slideshows.
  3. Set the right pace. Five to eight seconds per photo works well for gatherings where people are chatting. Ten to fifteen seconds works better for a focused viewing session where people are paying attention to each image.
  4. Run it during dinner. A slow photo slideshow on the TV during a family dinner is an effortless conversation starter. Set the timing to twelve or fifteen seconds, enable shuffle, and let it run in the background. People will naturally glance at the photos and share memories.
  5. Keep your Plex library organized. Good folder organization on the server translates to easier browsing in any client. Date-based folders (2024-07 Beach Trip) make it easy to find the right photos quickly.
