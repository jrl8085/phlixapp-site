How to Create a Family Photo Slideshow with Plex

Published September 1, 2026 · 9 min read

There is something special about seeing your family photos on a big screen. Holiday gatherings, birthday parties, vacations, everyday moments -- they all feel more vivid when displayed at full size rather than squinting at them on a phone. If your photos live on a Plex server, you already have the infrastructure for a family slideshow. The question is how to get the best experience.

This guide covers how to set up a photo library in Plex, the slideshow options in Plex's own apps, and how to run a big-screen slideshow with Phlix, an independent app that works with Plex Media Server.

Setting Up Your Photo Library in Plex

Before you can run a slideshow, your photos need to be in Plex. If you have not set this up yet, here is the quick version:

  1. Organize your photos on disk. Plex works best with photos organized into folders by date or event. A structure like Photos/2025/2025-12 Christmas/ keeps things clean and browsable. Plex reads EXIF data from your image files, so dates, camera info, and GPS coordinates are pulled in automatically.
  2. Create a Photo library in Plex. In the Plex Web app, go to Settings, then Manage Libraries, then Add Library. Choose "Photos" as the library type and point it at your photo folder.
  3. Let Plex scan. Plex will index your photos and generate thumbnails. For large libraries (tens of thousands of images), this initial scan can take several hours. Let it complete before trying to browse or create slideshows.

Supported Formats

Plex supports JPEG, PNG, TIFF, and several RAW formats (CR2, NEF, ARW, DNG, and others). JPEG is the most efficient for slideshows. RAW files work but generate larger thumbnails and take longer to process. HEIF/HEIC files from iPhones are also supported on most platforms.

Native Plex Slideshow Options

Plex has a built-in slideshow in Plex Web. Here is what Plex offers in a browser and on iPhone.

Plex Web App

In the web browser, open a photo in an album or folder and use the Play button to start a slideshow (Plex: Using the Library View). Photos advance automatically, and the Next and Previous buttons move between photos.

Plex Photos (iPhone and iPad)

On iPhone and iPad, Plex Inc. makes a separate app for photo libraries, Plex Photos. Its App Store page lists Recommended View, Timeline View and Library View as its supported features. To get photos from a phone onto a TV, you can turn on Screen Mirroring (see the note below).

A Note on Screen Mirroring

If you mirror your phone to the TV, the TV shows a copy of your phone screen. A 24-megapixel photo gets scaled to your phone's screen resolution and then compressed for wireless transmission, so the result is softer than the original.

Big-Screen Slideshows with Phlix

Phlix is an independent app that works with Plex Media Server, built for photo libraries. Here is what it brings to a family slideshow:

Big-Screen Slideshows on Your TV

This is the headline feature. Screen Mirroring starts from Control Center (with an Apple TV or an AirPlay 2 TV), and an HDMI adapter works too. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls.

Phlix asks your server for an image sized to the display, with hard cuts between photos. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen.

Shuffle and Timing Controls

Phlix lets you shuffle your slideshow so photos appear in random order, and you choose how long each photo stays up -- 2, 4, 6 or 10 seconds -- before you start. Want a relaxed pace for a family dinner? Pick 10. A faster pace for a party? Pick 2 or 4.

Chronological Timeline with Year Scrubber

Next to the folders on your server, Phlix presents your entire Plex photo library as a chronological timeline. A year scrubber along the edge of the screen lets you pick a year: tap 2019, and you are looking at photos from 2019. This makes it practical to browse libraries with tens of thousands of photos. Finding "the photos from that vacation three years ago" takes seconds, not minutes.

On-Device Slideshow (Free)

Even without an external display, Phlix offers a full-screen slideshow on your iPhone or iPad. This is included in the free version. Browse to a folder or pick a year, tap the slideshow button, and your device becomes a digital photo frame. Useful for a bedside table or a propped-up iPad on a shelf.

Save to Photos

Phlix saves photos from your Plex server into your iPhone's Photos app. This is useful for sharing photos, setting wallpapers, or keeping your favorites on the phone for when your server is not reachable. Your first 25 saves are free, and Phlix Pro offers unlimited saves.

Setting Up a Phlix Slideshow

Getting a slideshow running with Phlix takes about sixty seconds:

  1. Download Phlix from the App Store. It is free.
  2. Sign in with your Plex account. Phlix discovers your servers automatically.
  3. Browse to the photos you want to display. Use the timeline and year scrubber to find the right date or event.
  4. Start the slideshow. Turn on Screen Mirroring from Control Center (or plug in an HDMI adapter), then tap the slideshow button.
  5. Choose the options. Set timing and shuffle before you start, then let it run. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls.

The external-display slideshow is a Phlix Pro feature ($6.99/yr or $19.99 lifetime). On-device slideshows and all browsing features are free forever.

Tips for the Best Family Slideshow

  1. Curate an album first. Rather than playing your entire library, create a folder on your Plex server with your best photos from a trip, year, or event. A curated set of 50 to 100 photos makes a much better slideshow than 10,000 unsorted images.
  2. Use landscape photos for TV. Television screens are landscape (16:9). Portrait photos displayed on a TV have large black bars on the sides. Landscapes fill the screen edge to edge. If possible, favor landscape-oriented photos for TV slideshows.
  3. Set the right pace. Four to six seconds per photo works well for gatherings where people are chatting. Ten seconds works better for a focused viewing session where people are paying attention to each image.
  4. Run it during dinner. A slow photo slideshow on the TV during a family dinner is an effortless conversation starter. Set the timing to ten seconds, enable shuffle and loop, and let it run in the background. People will naturally glance at the photos and share memories.
  5. Keep your Plex library organized. Good folder organization on the server translates to easier browsing in any client. Date-based folders (2024-07 Beach Trip) make it easy to find the right photos quickly.
