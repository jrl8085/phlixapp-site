How to Use Plex Watch Together — Watch Party Guide

Published September 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Watching a movie with friends used to mean everyone had to be in the same room. Plex Watch Together changes that by synchronizing playback across multiple devices so everyone sees the same frame at the same time, no matter where they are. Whether you are hosting a weekly movie night with friends across the country or watching a show with a partner who is traveling, Watch Together turns your Plex server into a shared theater.

This guide covers everything you need to know: how to start a session, who can join, which clients support the feature, and how to work around its limitations.

What Is Plex Watch Together?

Watch Together is a built-in Plex feature that lets multiple users watch the same piece of content in sync. One person acts as the host, and everyone else joins their session. When the host plays, pauses, seeks, or skips, every participant's playback follows. Think of it as a virtual living room where the host holds the remote.

The feature works with movies, TV episodes, and other video content in your Plex libraries. It does not require any third-party browser extensions or screen-sharing software. Everything happens natively within the Plex ecosystem, which means video quality adapts to each viewer's connection independently. One viewer can stream in 4K while another watches in 720p, and synchronization is maintained regardless.

Requirements Before You Start

Before hosting your first watch party, confirm the following:

  • A Plex Media Server with shared content. The host's server must contain the media everyone will watch. Viewers need to have access to the library where that content lives, either as managed users or as friends who have been shared the library.
  • Plex accounts for all participants. Every viewer needs a free Plex account. They do not need Plex Pass.
  • A supported client app. Not every Plex client supports Watch Together. Check the compatibility section below.
  • Sufficient server bandwidth. Your server needs enough upload bandwidth to handle simultaneous streams for all viewers. Each 1080p stream typically requires 8 to 12 Mbps, depending on your transcode settings.

How to Start a Watch Together Session

Starting a session is straightforward once you know where the option lives. The exact steps vary slightly by platform, but the general flow is the same.

From the Plex Web App

  1. Navigate to the movie or episode you want to watch.
  2. Click the three-dot menu (or right-click the title) and look for "Watch Together."
  3. A panel will appear showing you as the host. From here, you can invite other users.
  4. Select friends or managed users from the list. Plex will send them a notification.
  5. Once at least one person has joined, click "Start Watching" to begin playback for everyone.

From Mobile Apps (iOS and Android)

  1. Open the detail page for your chosen movie or episode.
  2. Tap the three-dot overflow menu.
  3. Select "Watch Together."
  4. Invite users from your friends list or managed accounts.
  5. Start playback when everyone is ready.

From Smart TVs and Streaming Devices

On Apple TV, Android TV, Roku, and similar devices, the option typically appears in the context menu for a piece of content. Navigate to the title, access the options menu, and select Watch Together. The interface for inviting users may be more limited on these platforms since text input is cumbersome, so it is often easier to start the session from a phone or computer and then switch playback to your TV.

Inviting Friends and Managed Users

There are two types of participants you can invite:

Friends (shared users). These are people who have their own Plex accounts and whom you have already shared your library with. They appear in your friends list and can be invited directly from the Watch Together panel. They will receive a notification in their Plex app prompting them to join.

Managed users. These are accounts you have created under your Plex Home, typically for family members. They can also join Watch Together sessions, and since they are part of your home, they already have access to your libraries.

Participants do not need Plex Pass. The only requirement is that they have access to the library containing the content you want to watch. If a friend cannot see the movie in their Plex app, they will not be able to join the session for that title.

Supported Clients

Watch Together is available on most major Plex clients, but not all of them. As of 2026, the following platforms support it:

  • Plex Web App (any modern browser)
  • Plex for iOS (iPhone and iPad)
  • Plex for Android (phones and tablets)
  • Plex for Apple TV
  • Plex for Android TV
  • Plex for Roku
  • Plex for Smart TVs (Samsung, LG, Vizio)
  • Plex for Windows and macOS desktop apps

A few clients do not support Watch Together, including some older smart TV apps and the Plex HTPC player. If a participant's client does not support the feature, they will not see the invitation or the Watch Together option. The simplest fallback is to use the web app at app.plex.tv, which works on any device with a modern browser.

Playback Controls and Synchronization

During a Watch Together session, the host has full control over playback. When the host pauses, all participants pause. When the host seeks to a different timestamp, everyone jumps to the same position. This one-way control model prevents chaos where multiple people are fighting over play and pause.

Participants can still adjust their own local settings without affecting others:

  • Audio track: Each viewer can select their preferred language or commentary track.
  • Subtitle track: Viewers can enable or disable subtitles independently.
  • Quality: Each stream transcodes or direct plays based on the individual viewer's client and bandwidth.
  • Volume: Local volume control remains independent.

The synchronization works well for most content. Under normal conditions, viewers stay within a second or two of each other. Network latency can introduce slight drift, but Plex periodically resynchronizes to keep everyone aligned.

Limitations to Be Aware Of

Watch Together is a genuinely useful feature, but it has boundaries worth understanding before you plan a large event:

No built-in chat or voice. Plex does not include a text chat or voice channel during Watch Together sessions. Most people solve this by running a separate voice call on Discord, FaceTime, Zoom, or a regular phone call alongside the Plex session.

The host must remain connected. If the host disconnects, pauses their client, or closes the app, the session may end or become unsynchronized for all participants. Make sure the host has a stable connection throughout.

Server load scales with viewers. Each participant creates a separate stream from your server. Five viewers watching a 1080p movie means five simultaneous transcodes (unless all can direct play). Confirm your server can handle the load before inviting a large group.

Content must be in a shared library. You cannot Watch Together with content that the other person does not have access to. If you share only your Movies library but not your TV library, friends cannot join a Watch Together session for a TV episode.

Live TV is not supported. Watch Together works with on-demand library content only. DVR recordings that have been saved to your library work, but live TV streams do not.

Tips for the Best Watch Party Experience

After hosting dozens of watch parties, here are the habits that make the biggest difference:

  1. Test before the event. Do a quick one-minute test session an hour before your watch party to confirm remote access, bandwidth, and client compatibility are all working.
  2. Pre-buffer the content. Ask participants to navigate to the title before you start the session. This lets their clients begin buffering so playback starts smoothly for everyone.
  3. Use a separate voice channel. Set up a Discord server or group FaceTime call. The social commentary is half the fun, and Plex does not provide it natively.
  4. Limit the group size. While there is no strict cap from Plex, synchronization works best with smaller groups. Five to eight viewers is a sweet spot. Beyond that, you may experience more drift and higher server load.
  5. Hardwire the host. The host's connection is the most critical. Use Ethernet instead of Wi-Fi on the host machine if at all possible.
  6. Choose content everyone can direct play. If most viewers use Apple TV or iOS, H.264 in an MP4 container will direct play on virtually every client, reducing server CPU load to nearly zero per stream.

Watch Together vs. Third-Party Alternatives

Before Watch Together existed, Plex users relied on third-party tools like SyncLounge (now Plex Syncplay) to synchronize playback. These tools still exist and can be useful if you need features Plex does not offer natively, such as chat or more granular sync controls.

However, for most users, the built-in Watch Together feature is simpler and more reliable. It requires no extra software, no browser extensions, and no additional server to host. It works across every supported Plex client rather than being limited to web browsers. Unless you have a specific need that Watch Together does not cover, it is the recommended approach.
