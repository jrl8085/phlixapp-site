Best NAS for Plex in 2026 — Complete Buyer's Guide

Published September 1, 2026 · 11 min read

A NAS (Network Attached Storage) is the ideal home for Plex Media Server. It runs 24/7, uses minimal power, stores terabytes of media, and sits quietly in a closet. But not every NAS handles Plex equally well. The CPU determines whether you can transcode video, the RAM affects how many streams you can serve, and the drive bays determine how much storage you can grow into.

This guide compares the best NAS devices for Plex in 2026 across every price point, from budget two-bay units to enthusiast four-bay rigs with hardware transcoding.

What Makes a Good Plex NAS

Before looking at specific models, understand the three factors that matter most for Plex performance:

CPU and Transcoding

Transcoding converts video from one format to another in real time. When a client cannot play the original file directly (wrong codec, resolution too high, bandwidth too low), Plex transcodes on the fly. This is CPU-intensive. A NAS with an Intel Celeron J-series or newer can handle one or two 1080p transcode streams using Intel Quick Sync hardware acceleration. Without hardware transcoding, even a single 1080p transcode can max out an ARM-based NAS.

If all your clients support Direct Play (playing the original file without conversion), transcoding is irrelevant and any NAS with sufficient I/O will work. But in practice, at least some of your clients or remote viewers will trigger transcoding.

RAM

Plex itself uses relatively little RAM — around 200 to 400 MB under normal load. But the NAS operating system, background tasks (RAID syncing, photo indexing, Docker containers), and multiple simultaneous streams all add up. Aim for at least 4 GB for a Plex-focused NAS. Models with 2 GB can work for light use but will struggle under load.

Drive Bays and Storage

Two-bay NAS devices work well for personal libraries up to around 20 TB of usable storage (with two large drives in RAID 1 or SHR). Four-bay models give you room to grow past 40 TB and offer better redundancy options. Consider how large your library is now and how fast it is growing.

Best NAS by Category

Best Overall: Synology DS224+

The DS224+ is the most popular Plex NAS for good reason. It ships with an Intel Celeron J4125 quad-core CPU with Intel Quick Sync, enabling hardware-accelerated transcoding of H.264 and H.265 (HEVC) content. It comes with 2 GB of DDR4 RAM, upgradeable to 6 GB. Two drive bays support up to 36 TB of raw storage with current drives.

Synology's DSM operating system is polished and reliable. The Plex package installs directly from Package Center with one click. The DS224+ draws about 15 to 20 watts under load, keeping electricity costs minimal for a device that runs around the clock.

Best for: Most home users. Handles two to three simultaneous 1080p transcode streams or one 4K transcode with hardware acceleration enabled (Plex Pass required).

Price: Approximately $300 USD (diskless).

Best Four-Bay: Synology DS923+

If you need more storage capacity or want RAID 5 redundancy, the DS923+ is the step up. It uses an AMD Ryzen R1600 dual-core CPU, which is faster than the J4125 for raw processing but lacks Intel Quick Sync. This means software transcoding only, unless you add the optional Synology E10G22-T1-Mini 10GbE adapter with an Intel-based transcoding card.

The DS923+ comes with 4 GB of ECC DDR4, expandable to 32 GB. Four drive bays support up to 72 TB of raw storage, and you can attach a Synology expansion unit for up to nine total bays.

Best for: Large libraries, multiple users, and anyone who needs more than two drives. If most clients Direct Play your content, the CPU limitation on hardware transcoding is a non-issue.

Price: Approximately $550 USD (diskless).

Best Budget: Synology DS223

The DS223 uses a Realtek RTD1619B ARM CPU, which means no hardware transcoding and limited software transcoding capability. However, it costs significantly less than the Plus models and handles Direct Play streaming perfectly well. If your media is in widely compatible formats (H.264 MP4 or MKV) and your clients are modern Apple TVs, Roku devices, or smart TVs, transcoding rarely triggers.

Best for: Budget-conscious users with Direct Play-friendly clients and libraries under 16 TB.

Price: Approximately $190 USD (diskless).

Best for Power Users: QNAP TS-464

The TS-464 packs an Intel Celeron N5095 quad-core CPU, which is a generation newer than the J4125 in the DS224+. It supports hardware transcoding with Quick Sync, handles H.264 and HEVC with ease, and can manage three to four simultaneous 1080p transcode streams. It ships with 8 GB of DDR4, expandable to 16 GB.

Four drive bays, two M.2 NVMe slots for SSD caching, two 2.5GbE network ports, and HDMI output make this one of the most capable NAS devices in its price range. QNAP's QTS operating system is feature-rich but has a steeper learning curve than Synology's DSM.

Best for: Users who want maximum transcoding performance and expandability in a four-bay form factor.

Price: Approximately $500 USD (diskless).

Best Value Four-Bay: Asustor AS5404T

Asustor's AS5404T offers an Intel Celeron N5105 CPU (similar to the N5095 in the QNAP), 4 GB of DDR4 RAM, four drive bays, and four M.2 NVMe slots. Hardware transcoding is fully supported. Asustor's ADM operating system is less mature than DSM or QTS, but it is straightforward for Plex use. The Plex package installs easily and runs well.

Best for: Users who want four bays with hardware transcoding at a lower price than QNAP or Synology equivalents.

Price: Approximately $450 USD (diskless).

Which Drives to Buy

For NAS use, buy drives specifically designed for NAS workloads. The most common choices are Western Digital Red Plus (CMR) and Seagate IronWolf. Both are rated for 24/7 operation and handle the vibration of multi-bay enclosures better than desktop drives. Avoid SMR (Shingled Magnetic Recording) drives, which can cause severe performance issues during RAID rebuilds.

For capacity, 8 TB drives currently offer the best price per gigabyte. Two 8 TB drives in a two-bay NAS with SHR or RAID 1 give you 8 TB of usable, redundant storage. Four 8 TB drives in RAID 5 give you 24 TB usable.

Important: Plex Pass for Hardware Transcoding

Hardware-accelerated transcoding on any NAS requires a Plex Pass subscription. Without Plex Pass, Plex falls back to software transcoding, which is significantly slower and may not be viable on NAS-class CPUs. If you plan to transcode, factor the Plex Pass cost ($5/month, $40/year, or $120 lifetime on sale) into your budget alongside the NAS hardware.

Final Recommendations

For most people setting up their first Plex server on a NAS, the Synology DS224+ is the right choice. It balances price, power efficiency, transcoding capability, and software polish. If you know you need four bays, the QNAP TS-464 gives you the most performance per dollar. And if your budget is tight and your clients all support Direct Play, the Synology DS223 will serve your media just fine without the transcoding overhead.

Whichever NAS you choose, pair it with reliable NAS-rated drives, set up RAID or SHR for redundancy, and install Plex from the built-in package manager. You will have a silent, always-on media server that costs pennies a day to run.
