AirPlay Slideshow from Plex -- The Complete Guide

Display your Plex photos as a slideshow on your TV via AirPlay. Comparison of methods, quality settings, and compatible hardware.

September 1, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026

Showing photos on a TV is one of those things that sounds simple until you actually try it. Getting your Plex photos onto a large screen via AirPlay comes down to two approaches: you can mirror your screen from any app, Plex's included (and accept the limitations that come with it), or you can use an app that treats the TV as a true external display. This guide covers both approaches so you can choose what works best for your setup.

Method 1: Screen Mirroring

The most straightforward way to get Plex photos onto a TV is iOS screen mirroring. Open Control Center, tap Screen Mirroring, select your Apple TV or AirPlay-compatible TV, and then browse or run a slideshow in the Plex app. Whatever appears on your phone screen appears on the TV.

Advantages

  • Works with any app, including Plex.
  • No additional software required.
  • Immediate and familiar.

Disadvantages

  • Your phone screen is duplicated on the TV. This means the TV shows everything on your phone: the app chrome, navigation bars, status bar, and any notifications that appear. It is not a clean, full-screen photo experience.
  • Resolution is limited. Screen mirroring sends your phone's screen resolution (typically 1170x2532 for a standard iPhone) scaled to fit a 1920x1080 or 3840x2160 TV. The result is a photo surrounded by black bars, or slightly upscaled, and never as sharp as the original image.
  • Your phone is locked into the app. You cannot use your phone for anything else while mirroring. Switching apps or checking a message interrupts the slideshow on the TV.
  • Battery drain. Continuous screen mirroring consumes significant battery because the phone is actively encoding and transmitting video.

Method 2: Phlix during Screen Mirroring -- the TV shows only the photo

Screen Mirroring is how an iPhone puts an app on a TV over AirPlay, but an app can do more with it than show a copy of the phone screen. While Mirroring is on, iOS hands the app the TV as a second screen, and an app built for it can draw its own full-screen window there while the phone shows something else, like playback controls. That is the ideal approach for photo slideshows: the TV gets a clean, distraction-free photo display while your phone stays useful as the controller.

This is where Phlix, an independent app that works with Plex Media Server, comes in.

Phlix: Just the Photo on the TV, the Controls on Your Phone

Start a slideshow in Phlix. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen. In detail:

On the TV: Just the photo, full screen, with no app chrome, no status bars, and no notifications. Phlix asks your server for an image sized to the display, with hard cuts between photos.

Choose 2, 4, 6 or 10 seconds a slide, shuffle, loop and whether to include videos before you start. Closing the player ends the show on the TV.

Why This Matters

The difference between a plain mirror and Phlix on the TV is the difference between "photos on a TV" and "a photo experience on a TV." With a plain mirror, your audience sees the app interface. With Phlix taking over the TV, they see only photos -- the way a dedicated digital photo frame would present them, but pulling from your entire Plex library.

This makes Phlix a good fit for family gatherings, dinner parties, holiday visits, or any occasion where you want photos playing in the background without requiring someone to babysit an app. Start the slideshow, set your phone down, and let it run.

Quality Settings

Photo quality on the TV depends on two factors: the source image resolution and the output resolution of the connection.

Source Resolution

Phlix asks your Plex server for an image sized to the display, which the server makes from your original. A 24-megapixel photo from a modern camera (6000x4000 pixels) or a 12-megapixel phone photo (4032x3024) has plenty of detail for a big screen. Even older photos at lower resolutions look better on a TV than on a phone screen because the viewing distance compensates for the lower pixel density.

Output Resolution

The output resolution depends on the connection: over an HDMI adapter it is what the TV or monitor reports to iOS, and over AirPlay Screen Mirroring it depends on the receiver, commonly 1080p. In all cases Phlix asks your server for an image sized to the display, so the photo is as sharp as the connection allows.

Compatible Hardware

Phlix slideshows work with the following hardware:

  • Apple TV: Works over Screen Mirroring; the output resolution depends on the model.
  • AirPlay 2-compatible smart TVs: Samsung (2018+), LG (2019+), Sony (2019+), Vizio (2018+). Resolution depends on the TV's AirPlay implementation.
  • Mac as AirPlay receiver: macOS Monterey and later can receive AirPlay. Useful for displaying slideshows on a monitor or projector.
  • USB-C HDMI adapter: A wired connection to any TV or monitor.

iPhone requirement: Any iPhone running iOS 17 or later. Phlix uses the standard iOS external-display support, so nothing beyond Screen Mirroring or an HDMI adapter is needed.

Practical Tips for Great Slideshows

  • Use a wired network for the Plex server. A wired connection to the server helps each photo arrive in time for its slide.
  • Keep your iPhone plugged in. Screen Mirroring uses more battery than normal usage. Plugging in ensures the slideshow can run indefinitely.
  • Curate with a folder or a year. Start the slideshow in a folder of your best photos, or pick a year on the timeline first, to show a highlight reel rather than every photo in your library.
