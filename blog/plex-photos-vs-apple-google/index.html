Plex Photos vs Apple Photos vs Google Photos -- Full Comparison

Compare self-hosted Plex photos with Apple Photos and Google Photos. Privacy, cost, features, and where each one wins.

September 1, 2026

Where should your photos live? For most people, the answer defaults to whatever their phone offers: Apple Photos for iPhone users, Google Photos for Android users. But if you already run a Plex server for your media collection, your photos can live there too -- on hardware you own, under your complete control. Each approach has genuine strengths and real trade-offs. This comparison breaks them down honestly.

Self-Hosting: The Privacy and Cost Argument

The strongest case for Plex Photos is control. Your photos never leave your network. No cloud provider scans them, no terms of service change can restrict your access, and no subscription fee increases. Once you own the hardware, storage is a one-time cost -- a 4 TB hard drive for $80 stores roughly 800,000 photos at typical JPEG sizes.

The Real Cost Calculation

Self-hosting is not free. The hardware costs money upfront (a basic NAS starts around $200, plus drives), consumes electricity ($5-15/month for a NAS running 24/7), and requires occasional maintenance. However, compared to paying $2.99/month for 200 GB of iCloud storage or Google One, a NAS pays for itself within two to three years -- and then every subsequent year is savings.

For someone with 100,000 photos consuming 500 GB to 1 TB of storage, the cloud alternative would be $9.99/month for Apple's 2 TB plan or Google's equivalent. That is $120/year, indefinitely. A one-time NAS investment of $400-600 breaks even in under five years and then costs only electricity.

Privacy Without Compromise

With Plex, your photos exist on drives in your home. There is no cloud intermediary, no data processing agreement to accept, and no risk of a provider scanning your images for ML training data. Apple Photos with Advanced Data Protection comes close to this level of privacy with end-to-end encryption, but your data still resides on Apple's servers. Google Photos explicitly uses uploaded content to improve its services, which is a non-starter for privacy-conscious users.

Apple Photos: The Polished Ecosystem

Apple Photos is the most refined photo management experience available to iPhone users. Its strengths are substantial:

  • Machine learning. On-device face recognition, object detection, scene classification, and natural-language search ("photos of dogs at the beach") are genuinely useful and continuously improving. This is the feature Plex cannot match.
  • Editing. A full non-destructive editing suite with exposure, color, cropping, filters, portrait lighting adjustments, and markup tools. Edits sync across all devices.
  • Seamless sync. Take a photo on your iPhone, and it appears on your iPad, Mac, and Apple TV within seconds. No configuration needed.
  • Shared Library. iCloud Shared Photo Library lets up to six family members contribute to and view a shared collection automatically. No server management required.
  • Memories. Auto-generated highlight reels with music, transitions, and curated photo selections that surface forgotten moments.

The trade-off is cost (iCloud storage subscriptions add up over decades) and platform lock-in (accessing your full library on a non-Apple device is impractical).

Google Photos: The Search Engine for Your Life

Google Photos' standout feature is search. Google's ML models can identify specific faces, pets, locations, activities, objects, text in images, and abstract concepts with remarkable accuracy. Searching "birthday cake 2022" will find exactly the photos you expect, even if they have no metadata or tags.

  • Cross-platform. Google Photos works equally well on iOS, Android, web browsers, and Chromebooks. It is the best option for households with mixed Apple and Android devices.
  • Magic Eraser and editing tools. Object removal, sky replacement, and other AI-powered editing tools are impressive and improving rapidly.
  • Partner sharing. Automatically share photos of specific people (like your partner or children) with another Google account.

The trade-off is privacy. Google's business model is built on data, and your photos are data. Even if Google does not show you ads based on your photo content today, the terms of service permit broad use of uploaded content. The free 15 GB tier (shared with Gmail and Drive) fills quickly for active photographers, pushing you toward a paid Google One plan.
