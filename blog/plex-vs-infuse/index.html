Plex vs Infuse — Which Video Player Is Better?

By Jason Loomis · September 1, 2026 · 9 min read

If you run a Plex Media Server and use Apple devices, you have a choice: use the official Plex app or use Infuse by Firecore as your player. Both connect to your Plex server. Both play your media. But they approach the job very differently, and which one is "better" depends entirely on what you prioritize.

This is a practical comparison based on real-world use with both apps across Apple TV 4K, iPhone, and iPad.

The Core Difference: Transcoding vs Direct Play

The fundamental difference between these two apps comes down to one thing: how they handle media formats your device does not natively support.

The official Plex app relies on the server to transcode incompatible formats. If your video has DTS audio, PGS subtitles, or Dolby Vision Profile 7, the Plex app asks the server to convert the stream into a compatible format in real time. This uses server CPU/GPU, increases latency, and can reduce quality.

Infuse handles the decoding locally on the Apple device. It has its own software decoder for virtually every codec and container format, so it Direct Plays almost everything. Your server just sends the raw file, and Infuse decodes it on the Apple TV or iPhone. No server transcoding needed.

User Interface

Plex App

The official Plex app provides the full Plex experience: your library organized with Plex's metadata, collections, playlists, Continue Watching, Discover, Watchlist, and Plex's free ad-supported content. The interface is cohesive, well-designed, and familiar. Library browsing is fast, and the player controls are intuitive.

The Plex app also integrates with Apple TV's Up Next feature, so what you are watching appears in the TV app alongside other streaming services. For multi-user households, account switching is seamless.

Infuse

Infuse has its own library interface that fetches metadata independently from TMDb and other sources. It presents your media in a beautiful, art-forward layout with large posters and backdrop images. The interface is arguably more visually striking than Plex's, though it is also more opinionated -- you are browsing Infuse's interpretation of your library rather than Plex's.

Infuse does not show Plex collections, playlists, or Discover content. It cannot access Plex's Continue Watching or Watchlist. For users who have invested time in curating their Plex experience, this disconnect is noticeable.

Infuse supports Up Next on Apple TV and integrates with Trakt.tv for watch history syncing, which partially compensates for the lack of Plex integration.

The Apple TV Experience

Apple TV 4K is where the Plex vs Infuse decision matters most, because it is the primary living room player for many Apple households.

Plex on Apple TV is one of the best official Plex clients. It supports Direct Play for H.264 and HEVC, audio passthrough for TrueHD and Atmos, and HDR10/Dolby Vision (Profile 5 and 8). The interface is responsive, and Siri voice search works within the app.

Infuse on Apple TV is in a class of its own as a video player. The playback engine handles formats that the Plex app cannot -- DTS decoding, PGS subtitle rendering, DV Profile 7, and AV1 -- all without touching the server. For users with high-bitrate 4K HDR content and lossless audio, Infuse provides the purest playback experience available on Apple TV.

The trade-off is clear: Plex gives you the full Plex platform experience, while Infuse gives you the best possible playback quality and the least server load.

The official Plex app is free. Infuse costs money. For users whose content Direct Plays fine in the Plex app (mostly H.264/HEVC with AAC/AC3 audio and SRT subtitles), there is no compelling reason to pay for Infuse. The Plex app handles this content perfectly well.

For users with diverse libraries -- DTS audio, image-based subtitles, Dolby Vision Profile 7 content, or AV1-encoded files -- Infuse can save significant server resources and deliver higher playback quality. The $14.99/year cost is easy to justify against the electricity and hardware wear of constant transcoding.

Limitations of Each

Plex App Limitations

  • Triggers transcoding for DTS, PGS/ASS subtitles, AV1, and DV Profile 7
  • No native DTS decoding on any Apple platform
  • Photo browsing is basic -- no timeline, no year scrubber, no AirPlay slideshows (use Phlix for photos)
  • Occasional UI bugs that persist across updates

Infuse Limitations

  • Does not show Plex collections, playlists, or Watchlist
  • No access to Plex Discover or free ad-supported content
  • No Continue Watching sync with Plex (uses its own watched status or Trakt)
  • Costs money on top of any Plex Pass subscription
  • Does not handle photos or music -- video only
  • Apple-only (no Android, Roku, or Fire TV version)

When to Use Each

Use the official Plex app when:

  • Your library is mostly H.264/HEVC with AAC or AC3 audio
  • You use SRT subtitles (not PGS or ASS)
  • You value Plex collections, playlists, and Continue Watching
  • You use Plex Discover to find content across streaming services
  • You want a free client app
  • Non-technical family members need a simple interface

Use Infuse when:

  • Your library includes DTS audio, PGS subtitles, or DV Profile 7 content
  • You want to minimize server transcoding
  • You prioritize the highest possible playback quality
  • You run a low-powered server that struggles with transcoding
  • You do not rely heavily on Plex-specific features like collections or playlists

Use both:

Many power users run both. They use the Plex app for browsing, discovery, and content that Direct Plays well. They switch to Infuse for problematic files -- DTS audio tracks, PGS subtitles, or DV Profile 7 content. Infuse even lets you open files directly from the Plex app using a "Play in Infuse" action.

The Verdict

Neither app is universally "better." The Plex app provides the complete Plex experience, while Infuse provides the best playback engine. If you had to choose one, the Plex app is the safer default -- it handles most content well and gives you access to everything Plex offers. Add Infuse if and when you encounter files that trigger unnecessary transcoding.
