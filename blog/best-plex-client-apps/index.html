Best Plex Client Apps Ranked for Every Platform

By Jason Loomis · September 1, 2026 · 10 min read

The official Plex app is not always the best way to access your Plex library. Depending on your platform, content type, and what you value most -- codec support, interface design, or specialized features -- a third-party client might serve you better. This guide ranks every notable Plex client by platform, covering video, music, photos, and audiobooks.

The Official Plex App by Platform

Apple TV

The official Plex app on Apple TV is one of the best client experiences available. It supports Direct Play for most common formats (H.264, H.265/HEVC, AAC, AC3), has a responsive tvOS-native interface, and integrates with Apple's Up Next feature. Dolby Vision and Atmos passthrough work reliably on Apple TV 4K.

Weaknesses: Limited subtitle format support (PGS subtitles trigger transcoding), and the photo browsing experience is basic.

iOS (iPhone and iPad)

The Plex iOS app is polished and regularly updated. It handles video streaming well, supports downloads for offline viewing (Plex Pass required), and has a clean library browser. The app Direct Plays H.264 and HEVC content reliably.

Weaknesses: Photo browsing is functional but limited -- no chronological timeline, no year scrubber, and no dedicated slideshow features. For photos, a specialized client is significantly better (see Phlix below).

Android

The Android app mirrors the iOS experience closely. It supports a wider range of codecs thanks to Android's more permissive media framework, and it integrates with Android Auto for music. ExoPlayer handles most formats without transcoding.

Fire TV

The Fire TV app is based on the Android TV version and works well. Fire TV Stick 4K and newer models handle HEVC and HDR content. The interface is smooth, though the remote navigation can feel slightly less refined than the Apple TV version.

Roku

The Roku app is one of the oldest Plex clients and it shows. While functional, the interface is noticeably slower than other platforms and the codec support is more limited. H.264 is well-supported, but HEVC playback is inconsistent across Roku models. If you are buying a new streaming device primarily for Plex, consider Apple TV or Fire TV instead.

Web Player

The Plex web player (app.plex.tv) works in any modern browser and requires no installation. It is the most convenient way to access Plex from a computer. Direct Play support depends on the browser -- Chrome handles more codecs than Firefox or Safari. H.265/HEVC generally requires transcoding in the web player, which is a significant limitation for libraries heavy on HEVC content.

Samsung and LG Smart TVs

Smart TV apps exist for both platforms but tend to be less polished and slower to receive updates than dedicated streaming devices. If your TV supports it, use an Apple TV or Fire TV stick instead for a better Plex experience.

Best Third-Party Clients

Phlix -- Best for Photos (iOS)

Phlix is the dedicated photo browsing client for Plex. If you store photos on your Plex server, Phlix provides the experience that the official Plex app does not: a chronological timeline with a year scrubber for fast navigation through large libraries, 4K AirPlay slideshows for displaying your photos on a TV, and offline downloads to save photos to your iPhone.

The official Plex app treats photos as an afterthought -- no fast scrolling through years of photos, no dedicated slideshow mode for external displays, and slow performance with large libraries. Phlix was built specifically to solve these problems.

  • Timeline view: Scroll through your entire photo library chronologically with a year scrubber, similar to the native Photos app experience
  • AirPlay slideshows: Display your Plex photos as a 4K slideshow on your Apple TV or AirPlay-enabled TV
  • Offline downloads: Save photos from your Plex server to your iPhone for offline viewing
  • Performance: Handles libraries with 100,000+ photos without lag

Phlix is free to browse, with Pro features (unlimited downloads, external-display slideshows) available for $6.99/year or $19.99 lifetime. It works with any Plex Media Server and does not require Plex Pass.

Infuse -- Best for Video (iOS, Apple TV)

Infuse by Firecore is arguably the best video player for Apple devices. It connects to your Plex server and Direct Plays virtually every codec and container format -- including formats that trigger transcoding in the official Plex app. DTS, TrueHD, DTS-X, Dolby Vision profile 7, FLAC, and 4K HDR all play natively.

The result is less transcoding on your server, better video quality, and smoother playback. The trade-off is that Infuse has its own library interface that is separate from the Plex UI, and it costs $14.99/year or $94.99 lifetime.

Plexamp -- Best for Music (iOS, Android, Desktop)

Plexamp is Plex's own dedicated music player, and it is excellent. It offers gapless playback, loudness leveling, tempo-synced visualizers, time-synced lyrics (Plex Pass required), and a beautiful, album-art-focused interface. If you use Plex for music at all, Plexamp is mandatory.

Prologue -- Best for Audiobooks (iOS)

Prologue connects to your Plex audiobook library and provides a proper audiobook player with chapter support, sleep timer, playback speed controls, and CarPlay integration. The official Plex app handles audiobooks poorly, making Prologue essential for audiobook listeners.

Recommendations

For video on Apple devices: Use Infuse if you have a diverse codec library and want to minimize transcoding. Use the official Plex app if you prefer a more integrated library browsing experience and your content is mostly H.264/HEVC.

For photos: Use Phlix. The official Plex app is not a serious photo browser. Phlix provides the timeline, performance, and slideshow features that Plex photos deserve.

For music: Use Plexamp. It is in a class of its own for self-hosted music.

For audiobooks: Use Prologue. The official app does not handle audiobooks well.

For the best all-around experience: Apple TV 4K with the official Plex app handles most users' needs exceptionally well.
