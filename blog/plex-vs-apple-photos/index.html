Plex Photos vs Apple Photos — Managing Your Photo Library

By Jason Loomis · September 1, 2026 · 10 min read

If you have thousands (or hundreds of thousands) of photos, you have two fundamentally different approaches to managing them: let Apple handle everything in the cloud with Apple Photos, or keep full control with Plex Media Server on your own hardware. Both work. Both have serious trade-offs. And as someone who has run both systems simultaneously, I can tell you that neither is complete on its own -- but the gap between them is shrinking, especially with tools like Phlix that bring modern photo browsing features to Plex.

Apple Photos: Strengths

Apple Photos is the gold standard for consumer photo management, and it is hard to overstate how much Apple has invested in making it effortless:

  • iCloud sync: Every photo you take is automatically uploaded and available on every Apple device. No setup, no configuration, no thinking about it.
  • Search: On-device machine learning identifies people, places, objects, scenes, and even text in photos. Search for "dog at beach" and it works. Search for "receipts" and it finds them.
  • Memories: Apple automatically creates curated slideshows and collages from your library, complete with music.
  • Editing: Non-destructive editing tools built into the app. Crop, adjust, apply filters, and remove backgrounds without leaving the library.
  • Live Photos and video: Seamless handling of Live Photos, Cinematic mode video, and spatial video.
  • Sharing: Shared Albums and Shared Libraries make it easy to collaborate with family members.
  • Performance: The native Photos app handles libraries of any size with smooth scrolling, instant search, and optimized storage that keeps thumbnails on-device while full-resolution originals stay in the cloud.

Apple Photos: Weaknesses

  • iCloud storage costs: The free tier is 5 GB, which is laughable for photo storage. A family with 200,000+ photos will need the 2 TB plan at $9.99/month ($120/year) or more. Over five years, that is $600+ in recurring costs with no end in sight.
  • Vendor lock-in: Your entire photo library is tied to the Apple ecosystem. Exporting is possible but cumbersome, and you lose metadata, albums, and Memories in the process.
  • Privacy: While Apple encrypts iCloud data, your photos are still stored on someone else's servers. For privacy-conscious users, this is a non-starter.
  • No self-hosting option: You cannot run Apple Photos on your own hardware. Apple controls the infrastructure, the pricing, and the terms of service.

Plex Photos: Strengths

  • Self-hosted: Your photos live on your own hardware. A NAS, a desktop, or even a Raspberry Pi. No monthly fees, no storage limits, no third-party access.
  • No subscription costs: Once you have the hardware, photo storage and serving costs nothing. A 4 TB hard drive at $80 holds roughly 800,000 high-resolution photos. Compare that to $120/year for 2 TB of iCloud storage.
  • Full control: You control the file structure, backups, and access. Photos are standard files on a filesystem, not locked inside a proprietary database.
  • EXIF metadata: Plex reads EXIF data (date, camera model, GPS coordinates, focal length) and uses it for organization and display.
  • Sharing via Plex: Share your photo library with family and friends through Plex's user sharing system, just like you would share a movie library.
  • Integration with your media library: Photos sit alongside your movies, TV shows, and music in a single platform.

Plex Photos: Weaknesses

Here is where honesty matters. Plex's photo features have significant gaps compared to Apple Photos:

  • No smart search: Plex does not perform any image analysis. You cannot search for "sunset" or "birthday party" -- only by filename, date, or folder. There is no people recognition, object detection, or scene classification.
  • Slow with large libraries: The Plex web app and official mobile app struggle with photo libraries above 50,000 items. Browsing becomes sluggish, and loading thumbnails can take seconds per page.
  • Poor iOS browsing experience: The official Plex iOS app does not offer a chronological timeline for photos. There is no year scrubber. Scrolling through years of photos means endless manual swiping through paginated views. It feels nothing like the native Photos app.
  • No slideshow for TVs: The official Plex app does not provide an AirPlay-optimized slideshow mode for displaying photos on a television. You can cast individual photos, but there is no automated slideshow flow.
  • No editing: Plex is view-only. You cannot crop, adjust, or edit photos.
  • Camera upload is unreliable: Plex Pass includes a camera upload feature, but it has been plagued by sync failures, duplicates, and stalled uploads for years.

When to Use Each

Choose Apple Photos if:

  • You want zero setup and zero maintenance
  • Smart search (people, places, objects) is important to you
  • You edit photos frequently on your phone
  • You are deeply invested in the Apple ecosystem and do not mind iCloud costs
  • You have fewer than 50,000 photos

Choose Plex (with Phlix) if:

  • You want to own your photos on your own hardware
  • You have a large library (50,000+ photos) and want to avoid cloud storage costs
  • Privacy and data sovereignty matter to you
  • You already run a Plex server for movies and TV
  • You want to display photos on your TV as a slideshow
  • You share photos with family through Plex

Use both if:

There is nothing stopping you from using Apple Photos for day-to-day capture and editing while maintaining a Plex library as your long-term archive. Sync new photos from your camera roll to your NAS periodically (using Syncthing, rsync, or a similar tool), add them to your Plex library, and browse them with Phlix. You get the best of both worlds: Apple's polish for everyday use and self-hosted permanence for your archive.

The Bottom Line

Apple Photos is a better photo management tool in almost every way -- if you are willing to pay for iCloud storage and trust Apple with your data. Plex is a better photo storage and ownership strategy -- if you are willing to manage your own hardware and accept fewer features.

With Phlix, the browsing experience gap between the two narrows significantly. You get the timeline, the slideshow, and the performance that make Plex photos actually enjoyable to use on iOS. It does not replace Apple's smart search or editing tools, but it makes self-hosted photo browsing feel modern rather than like an afterthought.
