The Ultimate Guide to Plex Photos in 2026

Everything about Plex's photo features -- setup, supported formats, EXIF metadata, viewing options, and other photo apps that work with Plex Media Server.

By Jason Loomis · September 1, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026 · 8 min read

Plex is best known as a media server for movies and TV shows, but it has supported photo libraries since its early days. If you already run a Plex server for video, adding your photo collection means all your personal media lives in one place, accessible from any device, anywhere. The promise is compelling, and there is more than one way to view the result: Plex's own apps, and independent apps like Phlix that work with Plex Media Server.

This guide covers everything you need to know about Plex's photo capabilities in 2026: setting up a library, the formats and metadata Plex reads, and your options for viewing your photo library on Plex.

How to Set Up a Photo Library in Plex

Adding a photo library to Plex follows the same process as adding any other library type:

  1. Open the Plex web interface and click the + icon next to your library list.
  2. Select Photos as the library type.
  3. Name the library (e.g., "Family Photos") and point it to the folder on disk where your images are stored.
  4. Click "Add Library" and let Plex scan the folder.

Plex will index all images it finds, generate thumbnails, and read EXIF metadata. The initial scan can be slow for large collections -- a library of 50,000 photos may take an hour or more to fully process on a mechanical hard drive.

Folder Organization Matters

Unlike movies and TV shows, Plex displays photos based on their folder structure. Each top-level folder becomes an "album" in the Plex interface. A well-organized folder hierarchy directly translates to a well-organized library. A common approach is to organize by year and event:

Supported Formats

Plex supports the most common image formats:

  • JPEG / JPG: Full support including EXIF metadata.
  • PNG: Supported but no EXIF metadata (PNG uses a different metadata standard).
  • TIFF: Supported for thumbnails and viewing.
  • HEIC / HEIF: Supported on most modern Plex server versions. This is the default format for iPhones since iOS 11.
  • RAW formats: Limited support. Plex can generate thumbnails from some RAW formats (CR2, NEF, ARW) but viewing quality and compatibility vary by server platform.
  • GIF: Static display only. Animated GIFs are displayed as a single frame.
  • WebP: Supported on recent Plex server versions.

EXIF Metadata

Plex reads EXIF data from JPEG and HEIC files and displays it in the media info panel. The metadata fields Plex extracts include:

  • Date and time taken (used for sorting)
  • Camera make and model
  • Lens information
  • Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO
  • GPS coordinates (if geotagged)
  • Image dimensions and orientation

The date taken field is particularly important because Plex uses it to sort photos chronologically within each album. If your photos lack EXIF dates (common with scanned photos or screenshots), Plex falls back to the file modification date, which may not reflect the actual date the photo was taken.

The Viewing Experience -- What Plex Offers

In Plex Web (Plex's guide), you can:

  • Browse photos by album (folder)
  • View photos in a grid layout with thumbnails
  • Open individual photos in a lightbox viewer
  • Go to the next or previous photo
  • See a photo's details
  • Start a slideshow with the Play button

On iPhone and iPad, Plex Inc. also makes a separate app for photo libraries, Plex Photos. Its App Store page lists Recommended View, Timeline View and Library View as its supported features, and its latest version is 2025.2.0, from June 12, 2025.

Large Libraries

Photo libraries can hold far more items than movie or TV libraries, and a large one puts more load on your server: the first scan, thumbnail generation and the database all grow with the number of photos. Our guide to browsing 100,000+ photos on Plex without lag covers server-side tuning.

Another Option: Phlix

Phlix is an independent app for iPhone and iPad that works with Plex Media Server. It connects to your existing Plex server and is built for photo libraries. More on Phlix as another way to view your Plex photos on iPhone and iPad.

Chronological Timeline

Besides folders that match the folders on your server, Phlix presents your entire Plex photo library as a single, scrollable chronological timeline sorted by date taken. Every photo from every album appears in date order, much like Apple Photos. A year scrubber along the edge lets you tap a year, say 1987, to see just that year.

Built for Large Libraries

Phlix is built for big libraries: it loads a page at a time instead of everything at once. Server-side year filtering means each request covers one year, so the first screen appears before the whole library has been counted, and virtual paging means only the photos on screen are drawn.

Download to iPhone

Phlix lets you save photos from your Plex server to your iPhone's Photos app, one at a time. You get the original file when your server sends it; when it cannot, you get a copy the server converted, which may be smaller. The first 25 saves are free, and unlimited saves are included with Phlix Pro.

Slideshows on Your TV

With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. This is a Phlix Pro feature; without Pro, the TV mirrors your iPhone's screen.

Camera Upload (Beta)

Camera Upload (Beta) in Phlix copies the photos and videos on your iPhone or iPad into a folder of your Plex photo library on your home Wi-Fi, using a free helper you run in Docker next to Plex Media Server.
