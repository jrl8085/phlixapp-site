The Ultimate Guide to Plex Photos in 2026

Everything about Plex's photo features -- setup, supported formats, EXIF metadata, limitations, and the third-party apps that fill the gaps.

September 1, 2026

Plex is best known as a media server for movies and TV shows, but it has supported photo libraries since its early days. If you already run a Plex server for video, adding your photo collection means all your personal media lives in one place, accessible from any device, anywhere. The promise is compelling. The reality, however, has some significant gaps -- gaps that third-party apps like Phlix were built specifically to fill.

This guide covers everything you need to know about Plex's photo capabilities in 2026: what works well, what does not, and what your options are for getting the most out of your photo library on Plex.

How to Set Up a Photo Library in Plex

Adding a photo library to Plex follows the same process as adding any other library type:

  1. Open the Plex web interface and click the + icon next to your library list.
  2. Select Photos as the library type.
  3. Name the library (e.g., "Family Photos") and point it to the folder on disk where your images are stored.
  4. Click "Add Library" and let Plex scan the folder.

Plex will index all images it finds, generate thumbnails, and read EXIF metadata. The initial scan can be slow for large collections -- a library of 50,000 photos may take an hour or more to fully process on a mechanical hard drive.

Folder Organization Matters

Unlike movies and TV shows, Plex displays photos based on their folder structure. Each top-level folder becomes an "album" in the Plex interface. A well-organized folder hierarchy directly translates to a well-organized library. A common approach is to organize by year and event:

Supported Formats

Plex supports the most common image formats:

  • JPEG / JPG: Full support including EXIF metadata.
  • PNG: Supported but no EXIF metadata (PNG uses a different metadata standard).
  • TIFF: Supported for thumbnails and viewing.
  • HEIC / HEIF: Supported on most modern Plex server versions. This is the default format for iPhones since iOS 11.
  • RAW formats: Limited support. Plex can generate thumbnails from some RAW formats (CR2, NEF, ARW) but viewing quality and compatibility vary by server platform.
  • GIF: Static display only. Animated GIFs are displayed as a single frame.
  • WebP: Supported on recent Plex server versions.

EXIF Metadata

Plex reads EXIF data from JPEG and HEIC files and displays it in the media info panel. The metadata fields Plex extracts include:

  • Date and time taken (used for sorting)
  • Camera make and model
  • Lens information
  • Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO
  • GPS coordinates (if geotagged)
  • Image dimensions and orientation

The date taken field is particularly important because Plex uses it to sort photos chronologically within each album. If your photos lack EXIF dates (common with scanned photos or screenshots), Plex falls back to the file modification date, which may not reflect the actual date the photo was taken.

The Viewing Experience -- What Plex Offers

Plex's built-in photo viewing experience is functional but basic. On the web interface and most client apps, you can:

  • Browse photos by album (folder)
  • View photos in a grid layout with thumbnails
  • Open individual photos in a lightbox viewer
  • Navigate between photos with arrow keys or swipe gestures
  • View EXIF metadata for individual photos
  • Start a basic slideshow within the Plex app

For small libraries and casual viewing, this works adequately. The problems emerge as libraries grow and expectations rise.

The Limitations -- Where Plex Falls Short

Plex's photo features have received minimal development attention in recent years. The official Plex Photos app on the iOS App Store, last updated in June 2025, carries a 1.3-star rating. The core limitations include:

No Chronological Timeline

Plex has no unified timeline view that spans across albums. You cannot scroll through your entire photo history in date order the way you can in Apple Photos, Google Photos, or any modern photo app. Instead, you must navigate album by album, which is impractical when you have hundreds of folders.

Poor Large Library Performance

Libraries with more than 20,000 to 30,000 photos become noticeably slow. Loading album grids takes several seconds, thumbnail generation lags, and the overall experience becomes frustrating. Libraries exceeding 100,000 photos are essentially unusable in the native Plex interface without significant patience.

No Offline Downloads (iOS)

The Plex iOS app supports offline downloads (Sync) for movies and TV shows, but this feature does not extend to photos. There is no way to save a photo from your Plex library to your iPhone's Camera Roll through the official Plex app. This is a significant limitation for anyone who wants to share or use photos from their server on the go.

No Year Scrubber or Fast Navigation

When browsing large collections, there is no way to quickly jump to a specific year or date range. You must scroll manually through the entire library, which becomes impractical at scale.

Limited Slideshow Capabilities

The built-in slideshow is basic -- it plays through photos in an album sequentially. There is no external display support for a dual-screen experience (phone as controller, TV as display), and slideshow quality is limited by the client device's rendering capabilities.
