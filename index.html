Camera Upload (Beta): your camera roll, in your Plex library

Plex announced in March 2021 that it was ending its own Camera Upload. Phlix brings the idea back for iPhone and iPad: your photos go from your phone to your own server, and to no one else.

1

Install the free helper

One Docker container on the machine that runs Plex Media Server. You point it at a folder inside your Plex photo library. One command on Linux or a Mac, or let an AI assistant set it up.

2

Connect your iPhone

In Phlix, open Settings › Camera Upload. Phlix checks that the helper is on your network before anything is switched on, then pairs with it. The owner of the Plex server needs no code.

3

At home, it backs up

On your home Wi-Fi, new photos copy to your server: straight away while Phlix is open, and in short bursts while it is closed. Phlix shows what is backed up and tells you when everything is.

  • Only at home. Phlix uploads on your home Wi-Fi and nowhere else. Never over mobile data.
  • Only to you. Photos go from your phone straight to your server, encrypted. None of them go to Phlix.
  • Only copies. Nothing is ever deleted or changed on your phone. It is a one-way copy, not a sync.
  • Free to try. The helper is free, and its source code is public. The first 100 uploads are free, and Phlix Pro uploads the rest.

Save to Photos, or straight into an album

The photos and clips on your Plex server can go into the Photos app on your iPhone or iPad, where they stay whether or not your server is reachable. A file in a format Photos cannot store can go to the Files app instead.

📥

Save to Photos

25 free, unlimited with Pro.

Open a photo or video and tap ••• to save it. You get the original file when your server sends it. When it cannot, you get a copy your server has converted, which may be smaller. The app shows how many free saves you have left.

📁

Save to Album

Pick an album once.

Every photo you save from Phlix then goes straight into it. Find it under Settings › Storage › Save to Album, or tap ••• on any photo and choose Save to Album. Pick one of your albums, or make a new one. Choosing an album asks iOS for full access to Photos, because albums need it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Phlix?

Phlix is an independent iPhone and iPad app that works with Plex Media Server. It shows the photos and videos in your Plex photo libraries by folder or on a date timeline with a year scrubber, saves photos to the Photos app or straight into an album, and plays slideshows of a folder, a year or the whole library. With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls (Phlix Pro; without Pro, the TV mirrors the phone). From version 1.0.21, Camera Upload (Beta) backs up your camera roll to your own server.

What happened to Plex's Camera Upload?

Plex announced on its forum on March 18, 2021 that it was ending support for Camera Upload, with removal targeted for June 2021. Photos that were already uploaded stayed in Plex. Phlix, an independent app, has its own Camera Upload (Beta) from version 1.0.21: it copies the photos and videos on your iPhone or iPad into a folder of your Plex photo library, on your home Wi-Fi, through a free helper you run next to Plex Media Server. The whole story, with Plex's announcement.

How do I back up my iPhone photos to Plex?

Put them in a folder that one of your Plex photo libraries includes, and let Plex scan it. Camera Upload (Beta) in Phlix does that for you: install the free Phlix Camera Upload helper, one Docker container, on the machine that runs Plex Media Server, then turn it on in Phlix under Settings › Camera Upload. At home, on your Wi-Fi, it backs up new photos by itself. The first 100 uploads are free, and Phlix Pro uploads the rest. It needs Phlix 1.0.21 or later. If the App Store does not offer that version yet, it is on its way. The setup guide has every step.

Is there an alternative to the Plex Photos app?

Plex Photos is Plex's own free app for photo libraries. Phlix is an independent alternative for iPhone and iPad that works with the same Plex Media Server: folders and a date timeline with a year scrubber, slideshows that include subfolders, saves to Photos or into an album, big-screen slideshows on your TV, and Camera Upload (Beta). Phlix is free to browse, so you can try it on your own library. Phlix as a Plex Photos alternative.

Do I need Plex Pass to use Phlix?

No Plex Pass is needed on your home network. Away from home, Plex requires a Plex Pass (the server owner's or your own) or a Remote Watch Pass on your account to stream personal video remotely; Plex says music and photos stream remotely for free (Plex: Requirements for Remote Playback of Personal Media). Phlix itself needs only a Plex account and a Plex Media Server with at least one photo library. Camera Upload needs no Plex Pass either.

Phlix Pro removes those limits and adds big-screen slideshows on your TV: $19.99 once, with Family Sharing, or $6.99 a year. You can try Pro free for 14 days; the trial never renews and never charges you.

With Screen Mirroring on, or over an HDMI adapter, the TV shows just the photos while your iPhone shows the controls. Phlix is one app for iPhone and iPad; on iPad, a sidebar lists your libraries. On a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later, macOS 14 or later), the App Store offers the iPad version. Apple lists it as designed for iPad and not verified for macOS.

Is my data private with Phlix?

Yes. Phlix has no ads, no tracking pixels and no session replay. It sends anonymous usage, error and crash statistics tied to a random ID on your device, never your name, Plex account, photos, file names or server address, and you can turn that off in Settings. Camera Upload sends your photos only to the helper on your own server. See the Privacy Policy for full details.

What makes Phlix different from Plex Photos?

Plex Photos is Plex's own app; its App Store page lists a recommended view, a timeline view and a library view. Phlix is an independent app with folders that follow your server, a date timeline with a year scrubber, slideshows of a folder (with the folders inside it), a year or the whole library, saves to Photos or into an album, big-screen slideshows with Screen Mirroring or an HDMI adapter, a cellular data saver and, from version 1.0.21, Camera Upload (Beta).

Can I use Phlix outside my home network?

Yes, for browsing, as long as your Plex server allows remote access. Phlix connects to whichever server address is reachable, local or remote. For video, Plex requires a Plex Pass (the server owner's or your own) or a Remote Watch Pass to stream remotely; Plex says photos stream remotely for free (Plex: Requirements for Remote Playback of Personal Media). Some servers send only smaller copies of photos over the internet, so away from home a photo or a save can be a converted copy. Camera Upload is different on purpose: it uploads only on your home Wi-Fi.
